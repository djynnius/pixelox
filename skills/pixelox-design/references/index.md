Rules for a remix: one archetype, one palette, at most two attention-getting techniques.
If two picks both want to be the hero (a giant word and a busy collage), drop one. Never
rebuild one reference as it is: change the copy, the colors, or the imagery.

## 3. How to choose when nothing above fits

1. **Read the brief for four things**: what it is for (genre), how it should feel (mood),
   the format, and what material exists (a photo of a person, a product shot, a logo, or
   only words).
2. **Filter by material first** (section 5, "By what you must supply"). With no photo,
   use only the `nothing` row. Do not pick a photo-led reference and fake the photo.
3. Intersect with the genre and mood lists (section 5) to get 5 to 10 candidates, read
   their titles in [catalog.md](catalog.md), and pick 2 to 4.
4. Open those files and [techniques.md](techniques.md) for the IDs they cite.

## 4. Layout archetypes

Zone sizes are percent of the canvas height unless marked W.

| ID | Structure | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `A-event-stack` | Center axis. Presenter or logo 3 to 14%, headline 15 to 60%, hero subject 50 to 100% overlapping the headline, info bar 80 to 92% | church, concert, and speaker flyers |
| `A-speakers-over-title` | Presenter strip at the top, 1 to 5 cutouts side by side 10 to 55% on a glow, headline 47 to 75% across their lower bodies, details below | events with several speakers or artists |
| `A-side-subject-column` | Text column hung on one side (45 to 60% W); a cutout fills the other side and bleeds off the edge; bars or details across the bottom third | speaker events, fitness, corporate |
| `A-type-block` | Type is the image: a stacked headline fills 70 to 95% of the canvas, small details in a strip at the top or bottom | statements, sales, protest, type-only briefs |
| `A-text-behind-subject` | One giant word (cap height 25 to 45%) behind a cutout subject that overlaps it; small text at the edges | fashion, sport, personality, youth events |
| `A-word-with-object` | One giant word across the middle with an object set into or onto it (replacing a letter, breaking through it); small left-hung text | product launches, clever one-idea ads |
| `A-top-photo-bottom-text` | Photo fills 45 to 60% and fades into a flat field that carries the text block | conferences, corporate, cinematic announcements |
| `A-photo-panel` | A tall keylined photo or picture panel (50 to 70% of the area) with the subject breaking its edge; text hung against or under it | fashion, portraits of honor, profiles |
| `A-hero-object` | One product or object, 40 to 70% tall, centered on a flat, spotlight, or splash ground; brand mark and one line of copy | product, food, and drink ads |
| `A-text-left-product-right` | Left column 45% W: headline, sub-line, button, web address; product or model on the right, overlapping the column edge; mark top right | social product ads |
| `A-macro-crop` | The product is larger than the canvas, cropped to a detail; one tiny slogan in a corner | bold drink and beauty ads, teasers |
| `A-promo-stack` | Bands from the top: brand and offer badge, hero cluster, product range row, contact bar | retail flyers, price promos |
| `A-cover-masthead` | Masthead across the top 5 to 18%, full-bleed portrait under or over it, cover lines stacked at the left and right edges, optional border | magazine-style covers, profiles |
| `A-collage-pile` | One upright card or torn portrait with 3 to 4 tilted, cropped cards or scraps around it; program or caption text in the gaps | zines, retrospectives, programs |
| `A-swiss-asymmetric` | One or two left-hung columns, small type, a large empty area, one big graphic shape or cropped word | design, tech, culture, corporate |
| `A-info-sheet` | Headline top left, a mascot or object lower left, three stacked section blocks (label pill plus bullets) on the right | explainers, feature lists, school and tech handouts |
| `A-abstract-title` | A generated full-bleed background (gradient, ribbons, streaks, spheres); one big title in the calm area; details in the corners | tech, music, and corporate events with no photo |
| `A-centered-title` | Symmetric: one illustration or motif in the middle 40 to 60%, title above or below it, author or credit line at the far edge | book covers, film posters, minimalist ads |
| `A-film-one-sheet` | Star name at the top, hero cutout 15 to 75%, title across the waist, tagline, billing block and date at the foot | film, series, and theatre posters |
| `A-diagonal-repeat` | One figure repeated along a diagonal toward a vanishing point; text set in the leftover triangle or along the diagonal | thrillers, dance, sport |
| `A-scene-title` | A full-bleed illustrated or photographic scene with the title set into it (its color or texture taken from the scene) | film, novel, game, and fantasy covers |
| `A-split-field` | Canvas divided into two color fields (vertical, horizontal, or diagonal); type on one, image on or across the other | sales, comparisons, fashion, announcements |
| `A-vertical-type` | Rotated words run up the left and right edges and frame a central image | modern art, music, editorial |
| `A-geometric-cluster` | Abstract shapes form the picture (40 to 60% of the area); type is small and tidy beside or below | exhibitions, festivals, corporate events |
| `A-frame-card` | A card, panel, or bordered frame set inside a ground; content lives on the card | templates, product promos, announcements |
| `A-portrait-caption` | A stylized portrait fills the canvas; one caption line at the bottom | tributes, speakers, iconic portraits |
| `A-portrait-type-stack` | A mono portrait in the top two thirds; a left-hung stack of 2 to 4 heavy lines over its lower body, each line a different palette color | book covers, profiles, campaign posters |
