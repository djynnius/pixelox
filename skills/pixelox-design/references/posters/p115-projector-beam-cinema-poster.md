# p115: Projector-beam outdoor cinema poster

## 1. Concept

A season poster for an outdoor cinema: a huge inverted triangle, a projector's beam seen
head-on, pours down the charcoal sheet from the full width of the top edge to a point
three quarters of the way down, where a small cream diamond (the projector) sits on a
pedestal. The beam is one gradient from violet through magenta to orange, and inside it
float paler silhouettes of film characters and black city skylines; rough black brush
strokes and paint specks tear into its edges. The venue's name is set in white straight
across the beam's narrow waist, two words run vertically at the far left and right, and
all practical information sits in four tiny centered blocks in the empty lower corners.
It works because one simple shape is the picture, the light, and the layout grid at once.

## 2. Format and grid

* Portrait A-series. Center axis at 50.5% W; the composition is symmetric around it.
* The beam: top edge 3 to 97% W at 0% H, apex at 50.5% W, 77% H. Its sides are the
  poster's two diagonals; everything else is placed in what they leave over.
* On the axis, top to bottom: date line 4.7% H; title line 1 at 44.4 to 49.5% H; title
  line 2 at 51 to 53.8% H; projector diamond 75.3 to 79.6% H; pedestal 80 to 84.5% H;
  web address 90.7% H; rule 93.5% H; partner logos 95.3% H.
* Vertical words: at 9% W and 93.7% W, centered on 46.8% H, i.e. level with the title,
  in the charcoal outside the beam.
* Info blocks: two columns centered at 21.7% W and 79% W, each holding two blocks, at
  73 to 82% H and 84.5 to 88% H, in the corners below the beam's sides.
* The title is wider than the beam at its height (55% W of type against 35% W of beam),
  so its first and last letters stand on charcoal and its middle on color.

## 3. Layer stack

1. Ground: warm charcoal `#262023`, full canvas.
2. Beam: the triangle, filled with a vertical gradient: violet `#4a2a8f` at the top edge,
   plum `#961e5f` at 14% H, magenta `#d72255` at 30% H, red-orange `#e35838` at 42% H,
   orange `#ec8c1d` from 52% H to the apex.
3. Tonal silhouettes inside the beam, each only one step lighter or darker than the
   gradient behind it: a giant head and raised arm (upper third), a retro robot or
   microphone shape (middle), small running and dancing figures (lower third, in pale
   orange).
4. Skylines: black-charcoal tower silhouettes standing inside the beam near its upper
   left and upper right corners, and a smaller cluster at 27 to 35% W, 36 to 45% H.
5. Brush strokes: four or five dry, streaky strokes in the ground charcoal, 30 to 45% W
   long, slanted 15 to 30 degrees, crossing the beam's edges at 15 to 33% H, so the
   triangle's outline looks torn and painted.
6. Specks: 40 to 60 round paint dots in magenta, orange, and violet, 0.3 to 1% W across,
   scattered just outside the beam's edges.
7. Projector: a flat cream diamond `#f2eed9` 22% W wide and 4.3% H tall, its upper point
   touching the beam's apex; under it five short horizontal bars in brown-orange,
   narrowing downward like steps.
8. Date line: small white bold tracked caps inside the top of the beam.
9. Title line 1: 7 cream-white bold geometric caps, 55% W wide, cap height 5.1% H; the
   round letters have small diagonal cuts where the beam's edge would cross them.
10. Title line 2: 6 pale-pink bold caps, cap height 2.7% H, centered under line 1.
11. Vertical words: white bold caps, cap height 0.8% H, very wide tracking, rotated to
    read from the top down, one on each side.
12. Info blocks: four centered blocks of tiny white bold text, each with a one-word
    heading in caps and 3 to 8 short lines.
13. Web address: bold tracked white caps, centered; then a thin gray rule across 80% W and
    a row of 8 small partner logos in white and gray.

One geometric family, bold everywhere, in four sizes. Small text is made legible by
weight and tracking, not size: the info blocks are only 0.5% H in cap height, which
works in print at A2 but is too small for a phone screen (raise it to 1.2% H for social).

## 6. Imagery

No photograph. The beam is a `shape path` with a gradient; the things "on screen" are
flat silhouettes one tone away from the gradient, so they are discovered slowly. Use
cutouts of whatever the event shows (performers, film characters, dancers, instruments),
flattened with **T-silhouette** to white at 15 to 25% opacity or to the ground color at
20%, and clipped to the beam. Skylines place the event in a city; any local outline works
(hills, a bridge, cranes).

## 7. Effects and textures

* **T-geo-shapes**: beam triangle, projector diamond, pedestal bars, skylines.
* Gradient: `fx overlay --stops` on the beam, six stops, `--angle 90` (orange first).
* **T-silhouette**: cutout placed above the beam, `layer clip`, white `fx overlay`,
  opacity 20%.
* **T-brush-swash** in the ground color: two renders of `brush.js` (`bristles=70`,
  `dry=0.6`), rotated -22 and -28 degrees, laid across the beam's edges.
* Specks: `particles.js` (`shape=dot`) in the beam's three colors.
* **T-vertical-type** for the side words; **T-fit-width** for title line 1; **T-rules**
  for the hairline above the logos.
* **T-paper-grain**, amount 10, soft-light 30%.

## 8. Hierarchy and eye path

1. The beam (the only color), read from its wide top down to the point.
2. The title across its waist, on the way down.
3. The cream projector at the point; then out to the two info columns beside it.
4. The vertical words at the sides, the date at the top, the address and logos at the
   foot.

Cap heights: title 5.1 : second line 2.7 : date 1 : address 0.85 : side words 0.8 :
info 0.5, about 10 : 5.4 : 2 : 1.7 : 1.6 : 1.

## 9. Build recipe

`subject.png` is your image here: a cutout of what will be on screen or on stage; it
becomes a pale silhouette inside the beam.

```bash
pixelox render js scripts/brush.js -o stroke.png --size 560x150 --seed 2 --param color='#262023' --param bristles=70 --param dry=0.6 --param rough=0.8 --param passes=2
```

Change first: the title word (6 to 8 letters keeps it wider than the beam at that
height), the silhouette inside the beam, then the gradient's three hues. For screens,
raise the info blocks to `--size 1.6%` and cut their copy by half.

## 10. Variations and pairings

* Other "one shape is the layout" versions: an upright triangle as a spotlight on a stage
  (apex at the top, title in the pool of light); a circle as a moon or a record; a tall
  rectangle as an open doorway with the title on the threshold.
* Gradient runs: ice (navy `#14213d`, blue `#2f6bff`, cyan `#7fe3ff`) for a winter
  season; acid (green `#0b6b3a`, lime `#b7e21c`, yellow `#ffe94a`) for a music festival;
  one flat color for a stricter Swiss look.
* Light version: bone ground `#efe9dc`, same beam, charcoal type and strokes.
* Festival, club night, theatre season, product launch; landscape format with the beam
  thrown sideways from a point at the left edge.
* Pairs with T-pattern (halftone dots fading inside the beam), T-splatter in place of
  the specks, T-outline-type for the second title line, T-info-bar in place of the four
  blocks.

## 11. Pitfalls

* Silhouettes inside the beam in strong contrast (black or white at full strength):
  they fight the title. One tonal step from the gradient, 15 to 25% opacity.
* A title narrower than the beam at its height: it drowns in color. Let the first and
  last letters stand outside, on the charcoal.
* Clean vector edges all round: the poster looks like a pictogram. The dry brush strokes
  in the ground color, crossing the beam's edges, give it a hand.
* Body copy scattered inside the beam. All small text goes in the dark corners in
  centered blocks, each under a one-word heading.
* Vertical words set as stacked upright letters or in long phrases: rotate the whole
  line, 1 to 2 words, with very wide tracking.
