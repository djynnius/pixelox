# p043: Pale winter scene with maroon title

## 1. Concept

A church-event flyer made of one high-key photograph (a small white chapel with dark red
roofs on snow under a white sky) and a compact maroon title block floating in the empty
sky. The single idea: the type takes its only color from the one colored thing in the
photo, the roof, so title and picture read as one image, and two thirds of the page stay
almost white.

## 2. Format and grid

* Portrait 2:3. One vertical axis at 50% W; side margins 6% W for the footer line, 18% W
  for the title block (it is only 66% W wide).
* Zones: presenter line at 4% H; title block 18 to 42% H; empty sky 42 to 58% H; the
  building 58.5 to 92.5% H; footer line at 96.5% H.
* The title block is centered as a whole, but inside it the date hangs from the headline's
  left edge (18.4% W) and the script word and sub-line sit 1.5% W right of the axis
  (52.5% W): a slightly loose, hand-set feel inside a symmetric page.
* Text occupies about 25% of the height; the photo subject about 34%; the rest is air.

## 3. Layer stack

1. Photo, full bleed: sky from 0 to 90% H graded from blue-white `#f1f6fb` at the corners
   to near-white `#fcfdff` in the middle; a thin gray band of sea on the horizon at 90% H;
   snow below it.
2. Building in the photo (not a separate layer): a white chapel seen side-on, box 19.5 to
   77.5% W, 58.5 to 92.5% H; a steeple with a small cross at 71% W rises to 58.5% H; nave
   roof and steeple roof in maroon `#6a0f2d` to `#814d61`, half covered with snow.
3. Presenter line: tracked maroon caps, centered, 4% H.
4. Date: maroon, left edge at 18.4% W, cap top 18% H.
5. Headline word: maroon condensed caps, 18.4 to 84% W, cap top 20.5% H, baseline 29.9% H.
6. Script word: maroon formal script, 23.8 to 81% W, top swash touching the headline's
   baseline at 29.5% H, descending to 39% H. Its capital's cross-stroke runs up into the
   gap between the first letters of the headline.
7. Sub-line: widely tracked maroon caps, centered at 52.5% W, 40.3% H.
8. Footer line: address, web address, and phone separated by spaced hyphens, tracked maroon
   caps, centered, 96.5% H, lying on the snow.

Near-monochrome white-blue with a single deep accent. The accent is sampled from the photo,
not chosen; with another photo it changes (see Variations).

## 5. Text elements

| Role | Content pattern | Case | Font class (look-alike / local) | Weight | Cap height | Tracking | Leading | Align | Color | Position |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Presenter | "`[ORG]` PRESENTS", 3 words | caps | condensed gothic (Bebas Neue, Oswald 500 / DIN Condensed) | 700 | 1.9% | 150 | - | center | `#6a0f2d` | 50%, 4.1% |

| Script | one word, 7 to 8 letters | title | formal high-contrast script (Great Vibes, Allura / Snell Roundhand) | heavy cut | capital 8% | 0 | - | center | `#6a0f2d` | 52.5%, top 29.5% |

Two families: one condensed gothic used at four sizes with three trackings (0 at display
size, 100 to 150 small, 300 for the sub-line), and one script word. Every text is the same
maroon. The script is as wide as 87% of the headline and almost as tall as its capitals.

## 6. Imagery

One photograph does all the work: a low, wide view of a small building standing alone on a
flat white plain, horizon near the bottom edge, about 60% of the frame empty overcast sky.
It is naturally high-key (nothing darker than the roof), with no grading beyond a slight
lift. What to look for in a replacement: a subject that fits in the lower 35% of a
portrait frame, a featureless pale sky above it, and **one** saturated color on the subject.
If the sky is not empty enough, fade the top of the photo into the ground color
(T-photo-fade run upward). The subject is small: about 58% W wide and a third of the
height. Do not crop in on it; the emptiness is the design.

## 7. Effects and textures

* **T-photo-fade**, reversed: the ground color `#f8fafd` is solid above 44% H and clears by
  74% H, which guarantees a clean field for the title on any photo. A second, short fade at
  the bottom (91 to 97% H, 90% strength) stands in for the snow under the footer line.
* **T-script-overlap**, quiet variant: script and block word in the *same* color, no
  rotation, no shadow; the script only kisses the block word's baseline (overlap under 5%
  of its height). The contrast is in the letterforms, not in color.
* **T-fit-width** on the single headline word (65.6% W).
* No grain, no vignette, no shadows: any texture would dirty the white.

## 8. Hierarchy and eye path

1. The headline word (largest dark shape on a white field).
2. The script word under it, then the tracked sub-line: the eye slides down the block.
3. Across the empty sky to the building, whose roofs repeat the type color.
4. The footer line, then the presenter line at the top.

Cap-height ratio: headline 9.4 : script capital 8 : date 2 : presenter and sub-line 1.9 :
footer 1.3, about 7 : 6 : 1.5 : 1.5 : 1. Two levels only: the title pair, and everything else.

## 9. Build recipe

`photo.png` is your image here: a portrait or square photo with its subject in the lower
third and pale sky above. It is anchored to the bottom edge at full width. The `adjust
levels` line lifts an ordinary photo into high key; with a photo that is already pale,
lower it to `--gamma 1.1 --out-black 0`.

```bash
pixelox new -o jubilee.pxo --size 1080x1620 --fill '#f8fafd'
pixelox comp place jubilee.pxo photo.png --in-place --as Photo --size 1080x --at 0,100% --anchor bl
pixelox adjust levels jubilee.pxo --in-place --layer Photo --gamma 1.5 --out-black 70
pixelox adjust vibrance jubilee.pxo --in-place --layer Photo --vibrance -15
pixelox layer add jubilee.pxo --in-place --name SkyFade
pixelox fill gradient jubilee.pxo --in-place --layer SkyFade --from 0,74% --to 0,44% --stops '#f8fafd00@0,#f8fafdff@1'
pixelox layer add jubilee.pxo --in-place --name FootFade
pixelox fill gradient jubilee.pxo --in-place --layer FootFade --from 0,91% --to 0,97% --stops '#f8fafd00@0,#f8fafde6@1'
pixelox text add jubilee.pxo --in-place --name Presenter --content "YOUR CHURCH PRESENTS" --font "DIN Condensed" --weight 700 --size 2.6% --tracking 150 --color '#6a0f2d' --at 50%,4.3% --anchor c
pixelox text add jubilee.pxo --in-place --name Date --content "14.06.2031" --font "DIN Condensed" --weight 700 --size 2.75% --tracking 100 --color '#6a0f2d'
pixelox transform move jubilee.pxo --in-place --layer Date --to 18.4%,17.7% --anchor tl
pixelox text add jubilee.pxo --in-place --name Headline --content "COMMUNITY" --font "DIN Condensed" --weight 700 --size 10% --color '#6a0f2d'
pixelox transform scale jubilee.pxo --in-place --layer Headline --size 708x
pixelox transform move jubilee.pxo --in-place --layer Headline --to 18.4%,20.5% --anchor tl
pixelox text add jubilee.pxo --in-place --name Script --content "Jubilee" --font "Snell Roundhand" --weight 900 --size 12% --color '#6a0f2d'
pixelox transform scale jubilee.pxo --in-place --layer Script --size 620x
pixelox transform move jubilee.pxo --in-place --layer Script --to 52.5%,28.8% --anchor t
pixelox text add jubilee.pxo --in-place --name Sub --content "SUMMER EVENT" --font "DIN Condensed" --weight 700 --size 2.7% --tracking 300 --color '#6a0f2d'
pixelox transform move jubilee.pxo --in-place --layer Sub --to 52.5%,40.2% --anchor t
pixelox text add jubilee.pxo --in-place --name Footer --content "12 EXAMPLE ROAD, YOUR CITY  -  WWW.EXAMPLE.COM  -  PHONE 555 0100" --font "DIN Condensed" --weight 700 --size 1.8% --tracking 150 --color '#6a0f2d' --at 50%,96.7% --anchor c
pixelox export jubilee.pxo -o jubilee.png
```

Change first: sample the accent from your photo
(`pixelox analyze colors photo.png --k 6 --json`, take the most saturated dark color) and
use it for every `--color`; then the headline word (7 to 10 letters) and the script word.
After changing the script word, re-read its `bounds` and move the sub-line to 1% H below
its lowest descender.

## 10. Variations and pairings

* Sibling: p044 uses the same title block in white on a saturated blue-sky photo, with a
  paragraph added. Choose this one (dark type) for a pale photo, p044 (white type) for a
  mid-tone or dark one.
* Other accents taken from the scene: forest green from a pine, rust from a brick wall,
  navy from a door. Always one color for all type.
* Season swaps: a pale beach with one bright boat; a foggy field with one tree; a white
  wall with one colored door. The rule (small subject, big pale void) stays.
* Works for retreat invitations, carol services, wedding save-the-dates, travel teasers,
  minimalist real-estate ads. In a square format keep the title block at 14 to 44% H and
  let the subject sit in the bottom 40%.
* Pairs with T-rules (a hairline above the footer) and T-paper-grain at its lightest
  setting (amount 8, opacity 25) if the white looks too digital in print.

## 11. Pitfalls

* Picking a "nice" type color that is not in the photo (a blue, a black). The link between
  roof and type disappears and the page looks like text pasted on a stock photo.
* Enlarging the subject to fill the space. Keep the building under 60% W and leave the
  16% H band of empty sky between the sub-line and the roof peak.
* A busy or blue sky behind the title: maroon on mid-blue vibrates. Fade the sky to the
  ground color first (the SkyFade layer), or switch to the white-type sibling.
* Setting the script in a second color or rotating it. Here the pairing is tone on tone;
  the script must stay level and touch the headline, not float 2% below it.
* A footer wider than 90% W or tracked tighter than 100: at cap height 1.3% H it needs the
  air between letters to stay readable.
