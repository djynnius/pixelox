# p063: Cream and brown sermon flyer with ribbons

## 1. Concept

A Sunday sermon announcement in one warm color family: a near-black stacked headline hangs
on the left of a cream ground, a speaker in a cream suit is cut out on the right, and
folded dark brown ribbons cross the lower half carrying the date, time, and place. It
works because the suit and the ground are the same cream, so the photo dissolves into the
page and only the face, the hands, and the brown shapes stand out; the headline's last word
is twice the size of everything above it and ends the sentence like a drum hit.

## 2. Format and grid

* Portrait 4:5. Left margin 10.5% W; the right edge of the text column is 65% W.
* Two vertical zones in the top half: text column 10.5 to 65% W, speaker 60 to 100% W
  (bleeding off the right edge). The speaker's raised hand reaches into the column at
  60% W, 44 to 52% H, and almost touches the last headline word.
* Rows: logo 6.5 to 12% H; headline 15 to 50.5% H; ribbon and date label 46 to 69% H; info
  bar 75.5 to 87% H (full width); footer lines 91 to 94.5% H.
* Every headline line is justified to the same two edges (10.5% and 65% W). The bars
  ignore the margins and bleed off both sides.

## 3. Layer stack

1. Ground: warm white `#f4f2ef`, with large soft washes: tan `#c7a586` in the top-right
   corner fading out by 25% W from the edge, cream `#e6ded3` along the bottom, white in the
   middle. No hard shapes.
2. Logo: a thin dark ring, 7.3% W across, centered at 13.8% W, 9.2% H, with a small
   triangular mark and a tiny wordmark inside.
3. Ribbon tail: a dark brown parallelogram entering from the left edge and falling to the
   right; corners at `0,46.5%`, `27%,59.3%`, `24.5%,69.3%`, `0,62.5%`. Gradient `#5a1c0a`
   (left) to `#2a0501`. A 25% opacity copy of the logo sits on it at 10.6% W, 59% H.
4. Headline line 1: one 5-letter word, black `#020101`, x 10.5 to 38.6% W, cap top 15.3% H,
   cap height 7.7% H.
5. Headline side lines: two short lines (one 10-letter word, then two words), black, set to
   the right of line 1 in the same band, x 40.8 to 65% W, cap height 3.1% H each.
6. Headline line 2: three short words, dark brown `#2a0602`, x 10.5 to 65% W, cap top
   25.2% H, cap height 6.8% H.
7. Headline line 3: one 3-letter word, brown-black `#150201`, x 9 to 65% W, cap top 34.3% H,
   cap height 15.9% H, in a much fatter and wider face than lines 1 and 2.
8. Slices: five hairline diagonal cuts in the ground color across lines 1 and 2 and the
   side lines, each 2 to 3 px wide, leaning 45 to 70 degrees, as if the letters were cut
   with a knife.
9. Date label: dark brown box `24.5%,59.3%,38% x 10%`, its left edge slanted to meet the
   ribbon tail (the tail reads as the same strip folded backward).
10. Speaker cutout: head and torso, turned to the left, head top at 26.5% H, head between
    75 and 97% W, body running off the right edge and down to the bottom. In front of the
    date label (the microphone hand overlaps its right end), behind the info bar.
11. Bottom fade: ground cream from transparent at 88% H to solid at 98% H, over the suit.
12. Info bar, three segments that read as one folded strip: left `0,76%,28% x 10.5%`
    (gradient `#5a210b` to `#120102`, left to right, edges tilted 1 degree); middle
    `28%,75.8%,46.5% x 9.8%` (`#460e02` to `#1c0201`); right, a parallelogram rising to
    the right at 13 degrees, corners `74.5%,76.2%`, `100%,71.5%`, `100%,82.5%`,
    `74.5%,86.2%`, rust `#b0572a` at the top to `#3a140a` at the bottom.
13. Bar text: white; time in the left segment, venue and address in the middle, a two-line
    note rotated -13 degrees in the right segment.
14. Footer: two small dark lines at 91.7 and 93.9% H, left at 8.7% W, with a red rounded
    video icon (3.4% W) inside the first line.

Three voices: a condensed heavy face for the sentence, one fat geometric face for the
payoff word, and a quiet geometric sans for the small print. The headline climbs in three
steps (3.1, 7 to 8, 16% H): small words are literally small, the key word is huge.

## 6. Imagery

One event photograph of a speaker in mid-sentence, cut out: a man with glasses and a short
beard, cream suit, pale blue shirt, patterned burgundy tie, a hand-held microphone in his
right hand at 68 to 76% W, 48 to 68% H, and his left hand open toward the headline. He is
turned about 40 degrees to the left, so his gaze and gesture point at the type. Clean
edge, no outline, no shadow; natural color, slightly warm. Because suit and ground match,
the lower third of the figure needs no clean crop: it is faded into the cream
(T-photo-fade). The logo is a simple one-color mark in a ring.

## 7. Effects and textures

* **T-gradient-mesh**, light version, for the ground: tan, cream, and white blobs on warm
  white, blurred with sigma 50, the tan one pinned to the top-right corner.
* **T-fit-width**: headline lines 1 plus side lines, 2, and 3 all end at 65% W.
* Sliced letters: thin `shape line` strokes in the ground color drawn over the headline
  (not in the library; see the recipe). Keep them thin, 0.3% W.
* Folded ribbon: three bar segments and the tail are separate shapes with their own
  left-to-right gradients (`fx overlay`), darker where two segments meet, which is what
  makes the joins look like folds. The bars follow **T-info-bar** and the date box
  **T-label-box**.
* **T-photo-fade** at the bottom, ground cream, 88 to 98% H.
* No grain, no drop shadows on the type.
* **T-sliced-type** in [techniques.md](../techniques.md) is the tested library version of the cut-and-shifted headline; start from it when reusing this idea.

## 8. Hierarchy and eye path

1. Headline line 3 (cap height 15.9% H, the darkest and fattest shape).
2. Back up to lines 1 and 2 to read the sentence from the top.
3. The speaker's face, reached through his open hand.
4. Down the ribbon tail to the date label, then along the info bar: time, venue, note.
5. Logo and footer last.

Cap-height ratio: 15.9 : 7.7 : 3.1 for the headline steps, then 3.5 (time) : 2.9 (date) :
2.4 (venue) : 1.2 (small print). About 13 : 6 : 2.7 : 1.

## 9. Build recipe

`subject.png` (a speaker cutout that faces left) and `logo.png` (a one-color round mark)
are your images here. The original also has a faint logo copy on the ribbon tail, a
time-zone note under the time, and five slices instead of three; add them the same way.

Change first: the four headline strings. Keep line 1 at 4 to 6 letters, line 2 at 10 to 12
characters, and line 3 at 3 or 4 letters with round or wide shapes (O, D, G, M, W); then

26% H and the face stays right of 70% W.

## 10. Variations and pairings

* Other single-hue families with the same structure: navy (`#f1f3f7` ground, `#0b1a3a`
  bars, `#2f6bd8` highlight); forest (`#f3f4ee`, `#0d2a1c`, `#4f9a5b`); plum (`#f6f1f4`,
  `#2a0a26`, `#a04a8c`). Dress the speaker, or pick a photo, to match the ground.
* Dark version: ground `#1a0705`, headline cream, bars in rust `#a8562a` with dark text.
* Mirror it (speaker left, text right-aligned at 89.5% W) when the photo faces right.
* Conference talk, webinar, book launch, guest lecture: any "one speaker, one topic" event.
* Square post: drop the footer and the side lines, keep lines 2 and 3 and one bar.
* Pairs with T-paper-grain at low strength, T-distress-type instead of the slices for a
  rougher feel, and T-cutout-drop-shadow (soft) when the suit does not match the ground.

## 11. Pitfalls

* A speaker in dark clothing on this cream ground becomes a heavy block that fights the
  headline. Match clothing and ground in lightness, or change the ground color.
* All headline lines the same size: the sentence goes flat. Keep the 2 : 1 jump between
  the last word and the lines above it, and keep the side lines under half of line 1.
* Slices that are too many or too thick make the type look broken or unreadable. Three to
  five cuts, each under 3 px on a 1080 px canvas, crossing two letters at most.
* Flat brown bars look like tape. Give each segment its own gradient and let the dark
  ends meet at the joins so the strip appears folded.
* The bar note is rotated, so long text will not fit: two lines of 12 characters at most.
* A subject that looks out of the frame (to the right) pulls the eye off the page: flip
  the photo or mirror the layout so the gaze crosses the headline.
