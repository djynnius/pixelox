# i027: Sixty-year award ring around a hero cutout

## 1. Concept

A white editorial sheet about one annual award: this year's winner, cut out in full motion,
bursts through a ring made of every past winner, one small radial entry per year for sixty
years, and two small counts at the bottom say which clubs and which countries won most. The
reader takes away "he joins this long line, and here is where he stands in it". It works
because the roll of honor, normally a dull table, becomes a halo: the list is the frame of
the photo, it ends beside the hero's own head, and one orange-gold carries every date and
every count.

## 2. Data story

* **The ring: 60 entries, one per year, 60 consecutive years.** Each entry is a year, a
  two-word name, a club (1 to 3 words, sometimes two clubs with a slash), and a thumbnail
  portrait. No values: it is a chronological list (a radial timeline). Many entries repeat
  the name before them (runs of 2 or 3 years), and the hero's own name appears several
  times among the newest entries.
* **Three finalists:** the winner (the hero) and two others (the small cutouts).
* **Clubs with the most winners:** 4 counts; the leader is half as large again as the
  other three, which tie.
* **Countries with the most winners:** 4 single-digit counts; three tie, the fourth is
  one lower.

Relationships: order in time (the ring), two small rankings of counted units.

Placeholder data in the recipe (example data, invented: a table-tennis award called the
Amber Paddle, 1960 to 2019): 60 awards, one placeholder name per year ("Player D" to
"Player AN", 36 different winners, with runs of 2 and 3 years); the last three awards went
to Player A; clubs North 9, Harbor 5, Valley 5, Lake 5; countries Northland 6, Eastland 6,
Westland 6, Southland 5.

## 3. Information architecture

1. **Title and deck**, top left: the organization and year in black, the award's name in
   orange-gold, then five lines that tell the news (who was nominated, when the vote
   closed, who won).
2. **The two other finalists**, top right: small overlapping busts with name captions.
3. **The hero**, center: the winner, the largest thing on the sheet.
4. **The ring** around him, introduced by a three-line label and a year range at its upper
   left. It is read clockwise: the oldest entry sits just right of the hero's head (about 1
   o'clock), the newest just left of it (about 11 o'clock), so the list ends at the face of
   the man the page is about.
5. **A short colored note** right of the hero's body, inside the ring: one fact about him.
6. **Bottom left:** four crests with a count each. **Bottom right:** four rows of dots with
   a count each.
7. **Source tab**, bottom right corner.

The ring is the only module that needs a reading order, and it has no arrow: the years
printed outside every entry give the direction.

## 8. Charts and diagrams

### The ring: C-radial-list, two calls on one center

`scripts/radial.js` without `values=` draws a ring of spokes with the entries written along
them, reading outward on the right half and inward on the left half, so no label is upside
down. The recipe runs it twice with the same center (`cx=50 cy=50.64`), the same
`start=24 sweep=312` (a 48-degree gap at the top for the hero's head), `rotate=1`, `fs=15`
(the script sets spoke labels at 0.9 x `fs`, 13.5 px), and `weight=600`:

* **Names:** `r=32.2 inner=0.9`, so the hub edge is at 29% W, just outside the red circle.
  `labels=` holds 60 names, one per year, oldest first. Each entry is a 17 px spoke with a
  dot (`colors='#c4c4c4'`), then the name in `muted='#222222'` from 32% to about 38% W.
* **Years:** `r=43.3 inner=0.9` (hub edge at 39% W) and `labels=` with the 60 years. The
  dot at 41.2% W (`colors='#8f8f8f'`, 17 px) stands in for the round portrait, and the year
  follows it in `muted='#dd9a3c'` out to 45.5% W.
* `highlight=60 accent='#dc8420'` in both calls turns the newest entry's spoke and dot
  orange-gold: the hero's own award. Entry k sits at `start` + `sweep` x (k - 0.5) / 60;
  both calls log an anchor `bar1` to `bar60` at each dot with its `angle`.
* The two circle lines are `shape ellipse` layers on the ring center.

Simplified: the 60 round portraits became dots; the two-line label (name over club) became
one line; the years are set along the spokes, not horizontally; the three gaps became one
(a bust has no legs). For a full-length cutout that crosses the ring lower down, run each
of the two calls once per arc with its own `start`, `sweep`, and share of the labels: at
the original's 4.85 degrees per entry, `start=12.4 sweep=131` for the first 27,
`start=162.9 sweep=19.4` for the next 4, and `start=206.6 sweep=140.7` for the last 29.

### C-stat-row (clubs)

Four columns of crest, small caps name, bold count, with a hairline after every column;
column pitch 8.8% W. The counts are in the text color: they belong to no chart color.

### C-dot-rows (countries)

One row per category: the name, a row of as many dots as the count, the count in bold right
after the last dot. All rows start at the same x, so row length is the value, like a bar
made of beads. `scripts/waffle.js` with `layout=rows shape=circle gap=0.25`,
`values=6,6,6,5`, the four names in `labels=`, box `68.6,88.7,27.4,7.7` (four rows at a
pitch of 30 px, dots of 18.7 px), `colors='#eea63a'`, `fs=18`. The script draws exactly as
many dots as each value says.

Simplified: the original right-aligns its names to the dots, sets the counts at twice the
name size, and tints dot k of every row with the k-th step of a pale-to-orange ramp. The
script sets names and counts at one size and gives a row one color.

## 10. Effects and finish

* **Hatched frame:** `scripts/pattern.js` with `kind=stripes angle=45 cell=8 weight=0.4`
  under a white `shape rect`.
* **Fades on the busts:** `mask add --layer Finalist1 --gradient linear --from 0,250 --to
  0,318`.
* **Floor shadow** (full-length cutouts): `shape ellipse` in `#000000`, then
  `filter gaussian --layer Shadow --sigma 18` and `layer opacity --layer Shadow --value 25`,
  placed below the hero.
* **Hero mask (stand-in only):** the stand-in is a bust, so the recipe clips it with a
  U-shaped vector mask that follows the gold circle below the ring's center and is open
  above it: `mask add --layer Hero --from-path 'M 260 0 H 820 V 795 A 280 280 0 0 1 260
  795 Z'`. The head rises through the top gap; the shoulders end on the circle. With a
  full-length cutout, delete that line.
* Everything else is flat: no gradients, no outlines, hairlines of 1 to 1.5 px.

## 13. Variations and pairings

* **Any long ordered list around a hero:** 50 annual editions of a prize, the 24 hours of
  a day, a 30-day challenge, 12 months of a season. 30 to 75 slots work at this size.
* **Few entries (10 or fewer):** use C-cycle or C-hub-spokes: round nodes on a ring with
  horizontal labels, the hero in the middle (S-cycle-ring).
* **A straight version:** C-timeline for up to 12 dated events under the hero, when the
  format is wide.
* **Highlight a run:** give `colors=` one color per entry (in place of `highlight=`) to
  tint the spokes and dots of one repeat winner, one club, or one country in the accent
  and gray the rest; say what the tint means in the note beside the hero.
* **A ring caption:** set the award's name on the red circle with C-arc-label, under the
  hero (the lower arc), in place of the section label.
* **No photo:** put a hero number in the ring's center (C-stat-block: "60 winners") and
  keep the two bottom modules.
* **Dark version:** Civic dark from [../palettes.md](../palettes.md): ground `#122e39`,
  spokes `#24424d`, dots `#35596a`, accent `#e9e3a6`, white type.

## 14. Pitfalls

* **Sixty two-line labels at 9 to 11 px.** Fine on a printed page, lost on a screen. The
  recipe sets one line per entry at 13.5 px, the least that reads at 1080 wide; for a
  phone-first piece put 20 to 30 entries on a ring at `fs=20`, or cut the list into
  several sheets.
* **Upside-down labels.** Rotating every label by its spoke angle turns the left half on
  its head. `scripts/radial.js` flips the reading direction at 180 degrees by itself; a
  label you add yourself with `transform rotate` takes the anchor's `angle` minus 90 on
  the right half and minus 270 on the left.
* **A range that does not match the ring (data integrity).** The label beside the ring
  states a year range; it must be the ring's first and last entry, and the two `labels=`
  lists must have the same length in the same order, or every name slides to a wrong
  year. Count them: both calls log one `bar` anchor per entry.
* **Winners or awards? (data integrity)** A club's count can mean awards won or different
  people who won. With repeat winners the two differ. Say which one in the module's sub
  line, and use the same rule in both bottom modules.
* **Dots that do not equal the count.** One dot is one unit: never drop dots to make a row
  fit, and start every row at the same x. The original's tint ramp shows position only;
  readers take darker for "more important", so the recipe uses one color.
* **The cutout hiding entries.** Leave empty slots where the figure crosses the ring; do
  not let a leg cover three winners.
* **Real crests and portraits.** Club badges and player photos are protected: use neutral
  icons and initials unless the user supplies licensed files.
