# i031: Neon dashboard element kit on deep indigo

## 1. Concept

A sheet of about thirty small charts and widgets in one "control room" style: a deep indigo
ground, marks in hot magenta, violet, icy cyan, and periwinkle (often as a two-color
gradient), a few yellow and orange accents, thin light numerals, and darker discs and tracks
behind every ring and bar. It is a parts kit, not a finished piece: nothing is titled and
the text is filler. What it teaches is a system: four glowing hues, two gradient pairs, dark
tracks, no cards and no gridlines, and many chart kinds that still look like one family. The
recipe builds a dashboard from thirteen of its elements.

## 2. Data story

The kit shows chart **forms**; its numbers are filler. The forms and their data shapes:

| Element | Data shape |
| --- | --- |
| 3D columns | 4 categories, values 30 to 80%, one hue each, value above each |
| Concentric ring gauge | 4 independent rates as 4 arcs on one center; one headline count in the hub |
| Progress tracks | 8 values from 15 to 90% on full-width dark tracks, colored in pairs |
| Dense thin bars | about 34 unsorted values (a distribution or a daily log); two headline shares beside it |
| Small-multiple lines | 4 series of about 20 points, each in its own band, a share at each right end |
| Spike columns | about 40 values, no axis, 4 tick values under a dotted baseline |
| Arrow bars | 4 sorted values, 35 to 90%, value inside each bar |
| Ring cluster | 1 large share (about three quarters) and 5 small independent shares (10 to 100%) |
| Pie | 3 shares (about a half, a quarter, a seventh) and an unlabeled gap where a fourth would be |
| Ghost-track columns | 6 values from 30 to 90%, each on a full-height pale track |
| Layered areas | 3 series of 18 to 20 points, overlapping, plus one thin line |
| Year tiles, pins | 3 or 4 dated items with a short note each |
| Change indicators | 3 signed percentages, down in magenta, up in cyan |
| Stacked discs | 5 levels, widest at the bottom |
| World map | no data: a silhouette |

Placeholder data in the recipe (example data, invented, a design studio's week): columns
48, 76, 27, 63%; gauge arcs 72, 58, 81, 46% with 47 projects in the hub; tracks 31, 42, 22,
57, 70, 86; 31 daily values between 18 and 80 with headline shares 23% and 46%; pie 18, 30,
52; four lines ending at 38, 47, 24, 12%; three stacked series of 19 points; bars 84, 71,
58, 33%; ghost columns 62, 48, 91, 37, 26, 58%; rings 72, 55, 28%.

## 3. Information architecture

The kit sheet has no reading order: four loose columns of unrelated elements, top to
bottom, separated only by space. Read as a catalog:

1. **Column 1:** 3D columns with a boxed four-bullet key; spike columns; a pie in an outline
   circle; four icon discs; three change indicators.
2. **Column 2:** the concentric ring gauge; month dots and four pill meters; four lines with
   end values; four arrow bars; a 2 x 2 key; ghost-track columns; the world silhouette.
3. **Column 3:** eight progress tracks with three text columns; four year tiles; three
   location pins; the framed layered-area chart (two columns wide); pennant tags, a line on
   a grid, a jagged area.
4. **Column 4:** dense thin bars with two big light numbers; the ring cluster (one large,
   five small); the stacked discs with dashed leaders.

The recipe's dashboard gives the elements an order: a title strip; row A (columns, gauge,
tracks over tiles, dense bars with the two headline shares); row B (pie, four lines, the
framed area chart); row C (ranked bars over icon discs, ghost columns, map, ring cluster).

## 5. Layer stack

1. Ground `#251f43`, flat. No cards, no panels except the two outlined boxes below.
2. Darker holders, all `#1b1835` to `#1e1a3a`: pill tracks under bars, full rings under
   arcs, discs behind donuts (radius 1.3 x the ring's), the hub of the gauge. Unfilled ring
   parts are neutral dark gray `#333333`.
3. Marks, flat or in one of two gradients: **hot** magenta `#df0160` to violet `#9d00e6`
   (left to right on bars, along the arc on rings) and **cold** cyan `#82ecfc` to periwinkle
   `#6264bb` (top to bottom on columns and pills).
4. Accent marks: yellow `#fcf32b`, amber `#eca725`, orange `#e46923`, red `#e02b2e`, only as
   the later members of a color series (columns 3 to 6, tiles, small rings).
5. Outlines: a 2 px periwinkle frame around the area chart; a 1 px lilac `#5c5a8c` rounded
   box around the key; a 2 px cyan circle around the pie.
6. Numbers: light white for values that sit beside marks; heavy white inside rings, pies,
   and bars; periwinkle bold for axis ticks and change figures.
7. Filler text: tiny white paragraphs (9 px on the 1600 canvas, not meant to be read) with a
   colored bold lead-in word; in a real piece these become 18 to 20 px notes or go.

## 6. Palette

| Hex | Role | Area | Encodes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `#251f43` | ground | 78% | nothing |
| `#1b1835` | tracks, backing discs, hub; unfilled ring parts `#333333` | 5% | "the rest", the empty part of a scale |
| `#df0160` | series: magenta; start of the hot gradient; "down" in change figures | 4% | category 2; a decrease |
| `#9d00e6` | series: violet; end of the hot gradient | 4% | category 1 or 4 |
| `#82ecfc` | series: cyan; start of the cold gradient; "up" in change figures | 3% | category; an increase |
| `#6264bb` | series: periwinkle; end of the cold gradient; frames, map, axis numbers | 4% | category; structure |
| `#fcf32b` | accent series: yellow, with amber `#eca725`, orange `#e46923`, red `#e02b2e` | 2% | later categories only |

Categorical with a fixed order: violet, magenta, (red, orange, amber), yellow for warm
sets; periwinkle, cyan for cool sets. A gradient is never a scale: both of its colors belong
to one mark. Text is white, or periwinkle when it is secondary. Lime, green, and gray-blue
do not exist in the kit.

## 8. Charts and diagrams

Built in the recipe (all at `--size 1600x1430`):

* **C-3d-bars**: `scripts/bars3d.js`, box `3,7,20,27`, 4 values, one color per block
  (`#8700c7,#bd014e,#c04501,#d3c500`), `gap=1`, `floor='#1b1835'`, values above the blocks
  in light white (`numweight=400 fs=26`; a larger `fs` makes neighboring values collide).
  The script's view is isometric (parallel, 30 degrees above, the row running back to
  front from the upper left), lit from the upper left: each block's left face takes the
  given color, the top is 22% lighter, the right face 28% darker. **3D in the original:**
  four square prisms side by side on a common floor, turned 45 degrees so two side faces
  show, seen from about 15 degrees above; light from the right. Faces per bar (left, right,
  top): violet `#600191`, `#8700c7`, `#6e01a4`; crimson `#8b003b`, `#bd014e`, `#9b0241`; rust
  `#8e3201`, `#c04501`, `#a03a03`; olive-yellow `#9c9000`, `#d3c500`, `#b0a203`; a faint
  darker floor shadow. The depth encodes nothing: the value is the block's height, and the
  number is printed above it. Flat equivalent: C-columns in the same four colors.
* **C-ring-gauge**: `scripts/donut.js` with `kind=rings`, `cx=35.5 cy=21 r=10 inner=0.4`, 4
  values with their names in `labels=`, one color per arc, `trackcolor='#1c1836'`, `fs=16`:
  four arcs of 17 px on dark tracks, all starting at 12 o'clock on one scale of 0 to 100
  (a full arc covers 300 degrees), each with its name and value at its start; then a hub
  disc and two `text add` layers. The original's arcs are 7 px thin, unlabeled, and start
  at four different angles, which is why they cannot be read (see Pitfalls).
* **C-progress-bars**: box `49,9,23,13.5`, `track=1 max=100`, 6 values, colors in pairs,
  `trackcolor='#1b1835'`, `vlabels=0`, `gap=0.42`. The original adds a small dark knob at
  each bar's end.
* **Dense thin bars** (C-bars): box `75,9,13.5,24`, `dir=h`, 31 values, `vlabels=0`,
  `gap=0.5`, no names; then `fx overlay --kind gradient` on the placed layer gives all bars
  the hot gradient. Two C-stat-block numbers stand beside it.
* **C-pie**: `cx=13 cy=49.5 r=9`, `inner=0`, 3 values that add to 100, `labelpos=in`,
  `sep` = the ground color; a 2 px cyan circle 14 px outside. The original pulls one slice
  out and leaves a gap where a fourth slice would be (see Pitfalls).
* **C-sparkline** x4 (small multiples): `scripts/line.js`, `spark=1`, boxes 22% W x 5.6% H
  stacked from 37.5% H, `width=9`, the last value written at the line's end
  (`suffix=% numweight=400 fs=26`).
* **C-stacked-area** in a frame: `scripts/line.js`, box `50,39.2,46,21.8`, `area=stack`, 3
  series of 19 points, no axis, no grid, no labels, inside a 3 px periwinkle `shape rect`.
  The original overlaps three opaque areas, each from zero, so the back ones are partly
  hidden; stacking shows all three.
* **Ranked bars** (C-bars): box `3,67,20,15`, 4 sorted values, `vlabels=inside`, one color
  per bar. The original's bars end in an arrow point and carry the gradients.
* **Ghost-track columns** (C-columns with `track=1 max=100`): box `24.5,67,22,27`, 6 values,
  `trackcolor='#443f5d'`, `gap=0.12`, values on top in heavy white.
* **Ring cluster** (C-progress-ring x3): big `cx=81.5 cy=80.5 r=6.6`, small `r=3.2` at
  `cx=93.8`, all `inner=0.7 round=0 trackcolor='#333333'`, number inside in heavy white; a
  dark disc behind the big one.
* **C-world-map**: `scripts/geo.js` run with `render d3`, box `49,67,23,27`,
  `projection=natural`, `land='#6264bb'`, and `stroke='#6264bb' strokewidth=1.5` (borders
  in the land color close the seams between countries, so the map reads as one silhouette;
  `stroke=none` would also draw Antarctica). **Map spec:** region set world countries without Antarctica; projection
  Natural Earth (the original looks like Miller or Robinson: flat top, wide Greenland);
  encoding none: every country `#6264bb`; no legend, no labels, no insets; box on the kit
  `25%,85%,22% x 13%`. To use it for data, add `points='lon:lat:label:value'` with
  `pointcolor='#82ecfc'` (bubble areas follow the values) or `highlight=` for a few
  countries. Builds before 0.6.0: C-dot-map (`scripts/worldmap.js` with the same box and
  `points=`, `dot='#7376d2' fill=0.9`).

Described only (not in the recipe):

* **Spike columns**: about 40 round-capped columns 0.35% W wide, cold gradient top to
  bottom, no axis; a dotted baseline (cyan dots, four larger white dots) and four tick values.
  Nearest library form: C-columns with `vlabels=0 axis=0 gridlines=0` and 30 to 40 values.
* **Pill meters**: 4 vertical pills on dark pill tracks (C-columns with `track=1`,
  `radius=999`), cold gradient.
* **Stacked discs (3D)**: five flattened ellipses (width to height 3.3 : 1) stacked with
  growing width, 12% W at the top to 17.7% W at the bottom, seen from about 20 degrees above;
  top disc hot gradient `#c8008d` to `#9d00e6`, then `#b5024f`, `#721c50`, `#545074`,
  `#2f375a`, each with a soft shadow on the one below; white dashed leaders with one elbow
  to notes. Depth and tilt encode nothing; it is a five-level pyramid. Flat equivalent:
  C-pyramid with those five colors.
* **Location pins, pennant tags, a line on a grid with a value bubble, a jagged area with a
  pin**: decoration around a date or one value; use C-timeline, C-ribbon-label, C-line, and
  C-callout.

## 9. Icons and illustration

* Icon discs: 72 px discs with the hot gradient (left to right), white icons 38 px:
  `user`, `users`, `globe`, `map-pin` (the original: filled silhouettes of a man in a tie, a
  woman, a wire globe, a pin).
* Year tiles: 80 px rounded squares (radius 18) with an 8 px darker edge of the same hue:
  `#fbf27f` on `#e6d400`, `#f58c56` on `#e2570a`, `#e02c7e` on `#c20058`, `#b247e7` on
  `#9300d6`; the original adds a tiny white chevron under the year.
* Change indicators: a small triangle (`shape polygon`, 3 sides) pointing down in magenta
  or up in cyan, then the signed figure.
* No pictures, no photos.

## 10. Effects and finish

* **Gradients inside marks** are the kit's signature. The chart scripts draw flat colors;
  for a chart with no labels in it, place it and run `fx overlay --layer NAME --kind gradient
  --stops '#df0160@0,#9d00e6@1' --angle 0` (the overlay recolors every pixel of that layer,
  so render value labels separately or keep them off). On shapes, the same overlay; copy it
  to siblings with `fx copy`.
* A faint outer glow would suit the arcs (`fx outer-glow`), but the original has none: its
  "neon" comes from saturated color on a dark ground, not from blur.
* No shadows except under the 3D bars and the stacked discs. Round caps on arcs and thin
  bars; square ends on thick rings.

## 11. Hierarchy and eye path

In the recipe's dashboard:

1. The four tall columns (top left, the largest solid colors) and the ring gauge with its
   cyan number.
2. The framed area chart (the largest single element, the only frame).
3. The magenta ring with its heavy white number, bottom right.
4. Pie, ranked bars, ghost columns: heavy white numbers inside saturated marks.
5. Lines, tracks, tiles, dense bars, map: texture and context.

Heavy white numbers 54 : 34 : 25; light numbers 62 : 34 : 26; notes 16.

## 13. Variations and pairings

* **A real dashboard:** six to nine of these elements, each with a 20 px caps title in
  periwinkle above it, in three rows; one element (the framed area chart or the big ring)
  twice the size of the rest.
* **Portrait report (1080 x 1350):** title, the ring gauge as the hero at 50% W, a C-stat-row
  of three light numbers, the ranked bars, and the ghost columns.
* **One gradient only:** drop yellow, orange, and red, and use the hot pair for "ours" and
  the cold pair for "theirs" in a comparison.
* **Light version:** ground `#f4f3fb`, tracks `#e3e1f3`, the same four hues one step darker
  (`#c10052`, `#7d00c0`, `#17b6d0`, `#4a4cae`), text `#251f43`.
* Pairs with the palette "Neon board" in [../palettes.md](../palettes.md), with C-multi-line
  on the same ground, and with C-table for exact figures in a dark panel.

## 14. Pitfalls

* **Depth that distorts.** On the original's 3D columns and stacked discs the eye cannot
  tell whether the front edge, the back edge, or the volume is the value. C-3d-bars keeps a
  parallel view, so heights stay proportional, and prints every value: use it once per
  board, never with perspective, and build everything else flat (the discs as C-pyramid).
* **A pie with a hole in it.** The original's slices stop short of the full circle and one
  is pulled out, so angles no longer read as shares. Slices must add to 100; label the remainder "Other" in
  gray instead of leaving a gap.
* **Gradients read as scales.** A bar that runs from magenta to violet suggests that the
  color means something along its length. Use each gradient as one identity and never put a
  gradient legend next to it.
* **Rings on one center with different meanings.** Four concentric arcs look like one
  chart, but each has its own base, and an outer arc is longer than an inner one for the
  same value. Name each arc and print its value (C-ring-gauge does both at the arc's
  start), start all arcs at one angle, or use a row of separate rings.
* **Overlapping areas hide data.** Opaque areas drawn from zero cover each other; stack them
  (when they are parts of a total) or use lines.
* **Too many accents.** With eight hues available, a board turns into confetti. Pick four,
  and keep the same hue for the same thing in every element.
* **Unreadable notes.** The kit's 9 px filler is a pattern, not a size: notes under 16 px on
  a dark ground vanish. Cut the words instead.
