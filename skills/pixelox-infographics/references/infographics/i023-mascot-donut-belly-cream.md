# i023: Cream food explainer with a donut-belly mascot

## 1. Concept

A poster-format explainer on a way of eating, drawn in one family of olive, lime, and
leaf greens on cream. A big near-black fruit character with a smiling face, raised hands, and
sneakers fills the right two thirds, and the hero chart, a donut of reasons, is its belly:
the dark body is the donut's frame and its hub. Around the character sit small quiet
modules: three little donuts in a left column, an icon list of five types, three label bars,
and three tiny illustrated modules along the bottom. The message is "here is who does this,
why, and what it does for you". It works because the mascot gives eight unrelated modules
one center of gravity, and because one hue family keeps a page with six charts calm.

## 2. Data story

* **Three small part-to-whole splits** of one country's population, one donut each:
  experience with the diet (3 groups: one dominant slice near 90%, one near 10%, a sliver),
  political leaning (3 groups: roughly a half, a third, a seventh), and income band (4 groups;
  in the original the printed numbers are small rates per group that do not add up to 100,
  although the ring is full: see Pitfalls).
* **One hero split of 9 reasons**, shares between roughly 2% and 19%: five large ones of 14 to
  19% and four small ones of 2 to 6%. Six are labeled inside the ring, the three smallest
  outside on leaders.
* **Five named types**, each with a definition of 6 to 12 words: text only, no numbers.
* **Three unrelated percentages** (a high one near three quarters, a middle one, a low one),
  each a "how much lower" rate for one risk, drawn as three bars on one 0 to 100 scale.
* **Two single values** with a unit (between 6 and 10), one per sex, on two small flags.
* **One paragraph** of roughly 55 words, and **one word list** of 8 short items.

Placeholder data in the recipe (example data, invented; the subject is kitchen gardens):

## 3. Information architecture

1. **Title**, centered: a small line of wide caps (a five-word lead-in that ends in a
   preposition), then the subject as one huge hand-drawn word.
2. **Left column, top**: a section label and three small donuts stacked, each with a
   two-line title under it. They answer "who?".
3. **Mascot with the hero donut**: the largest thing on the page. The donut answers "why?";
   its title sits in the dark hub.
4. **Left column, bottom**: a second section label and an icon list of five types ("which
   kinds?").
5. **Bottom row**, three small modules read left to right: a question with three label bars
   and a still life of vegetables ("what do I gain?"); two small figures holding value flags
   over a paragraph on a green panel; a pepper character carrying a word list, standing on a
   ribbon.

There are no arrows, numbers, or bands. Size does the leading (title, mascot, donut), and the
two section labels at the left edge split the column into "numbers" and "definitions".

## 8. Charts and diagrams

* **C-donut**, three small ones: `scripts/donut.js` placed by center, `cx=19.7`, `cy=22.2`,
  `35.7`, `49.3`, `r=5.9`, `inner=0.56`, `sep=none`, `labelpos=out`, `fs=18`, `ink` and
  `muted` both `#2e2d0e`, and `font=` set to the geometric stack. Three or four slices each.
  No axis, no legend: every slice is named beside the ring with its share. The original
  writes the name over the number in tiny regular type with no leaders; the script writes a
  bold number over the name with a short leader. The third donut uses `start=30` so that no
  label lands on the title below it.
* **C-donut**, hero: `cx=62.9 cy=51.1 r=18.4 inner=0.54 sep=none labelpos=none`. The
  original has **9 slices**; the library limit is 5, so the recipe draws 5
  (`values=28,9,7,32,24`, `start=-30`): two large pale ones at the top and left, two small
  ones at 3 and 4 o'clock for the leaders, and the largest, darkest one at the bottom.
  Labels are separate `text add` layers centered on the logged anchors `slice1`, `slice4`,
  `slice5` (name on one or two lines, the share under it). The hole is left empty so the
  body shows through as the hub, and the title is set on it.
* **C-callout** for the two small slices: a dot on the slice anchor (`slice2`, `slice3`), a
  3 px leader with one elbow that crosses the dark belly, a dot at its end, and a
  three-line label on the cream ground. The original starts each leader on the dark ring
  just outside the slice.
* **C-bars**: `scripts/bars.js`, `dir=h`, `values=64,38,21`, `max=100`, `vlabels=0`,
  `radius=0`, `gap=0.255`, box `5.3,88.9,19.8,9.44`, no `labels=`. Bars start at one left
  edge and share one scale (100 would be 19.8% W). No axis, no gridlines. The names are one
  `text add` layer set **on** the bars with `--leading 51` (the bar pitch), the values a
  second layer with the same leading. `muted='#efeed0'` hides the zero line the script draws
  at the left edge. In the original each value follows its own label; the recipe sets the
  values in a column, which needs no measuring. The script's own `labelpos=inside`
  (C-label-bars) is not used here: it writes a name inside a bar only when the bar is longer
  than the name and moves it behind the bar otherwise, and on this 19.8% W scale all three
  names would land outside. The original lets every name start inside its bar and run past
  the bar's end, which only a text layer over the bars does.
* **C-icon-grid** as a one-column list: five rows of icon, heading, note. All headings are
  one text layer with `--leading 78` (the row pitch); all notes are one layer with
  `--leading 26` and a blank line between items, so three lines make one row.
* **C-ribbon-label** under the pepper; **C-title-block**, centered variant, for the head;
  **C-source-line** in the recipe only.
* The two value flags are plain pennants (`shape path`, 74 x 38 px, a notch at the free end)
  on 4 px poles. They carry a number each and are not drawn to scale.

Simplified against the original: 9 hero slices became 5; the rotated section labels are
horizontal; bar values sit in a column; the paragraph, the word list, and the ribbon text are
shorter because nothing is set under 15 px.

## 10. Effects and finish

* Paper grain over everything: `filter grain --as-layer --name Paper --amount 10`. The
  original also has a lighter mottling inside the large dark shape.
* Face shading: `fx overlay --layer Face --kind gradient --stops '#a9a810@0.35,#857a08@1'
  --angle 315` (light at the upper left, darker at the lower right).
* Panel corners: radius 10 px. Bars and ribbon: square corners.
* No shadows, no outlines, no separators between slices: neighbors differ in tone only.

## 11. Hierarchy and eye path

1. The title word: five times larger than anything else, dark on cream.
2. The mascot's face, then down the dark body to the donut, the lightest thing inside the
   darkest thing. The hub title is read here.
3. The leader labels at the right, then back to the left column: three donuts, top to bottom.
4. The icon list, by its hand-drawn headings.
5. The bottom row, left to right: bars, flags and panel, pepper and ribbon.

Type levels: 180 px title word, 38 px kicker, 27 to 30 px hand-drawn headings, 24 px hub
title, 18 to 22 px labels and notes.

Notes for reuse:

* **Hero donut first.** Change `values=` and `colors=` (5 at most), then read the anchors
  again with `--json`: here `slice1` 733,684, `slice2` 832,820, `slice3` 817,894, `slice4`
  663,980, `slice5` 533,784. Move the in-ring labels (`S1`, `S4`, `S5`), the leader starts,
  and the first two dots to them. A slice under 10% gets a leader label; anything larger
  takes its label inside (three lines of 18 px fit the 92 px ring).
* **Small donuts:** change `values=`, `labels=`, and the title under each. If a label falls
  on a title, turn that donut with `start=`.
* **Bars:** change `values=` and the two text layers together, in the same order. Keep
  `max=100` for percentages so the three bars stay comparable.
* **List:** the headings layer has one line per row; the notes layer has two lines and a
  blank line per row (write a one-line note as one line and two blank lines, except the
  last). Notes are at most 30 characters per line here, because the belly comes close.
* **Mascot:** the body, the face, and the limbs are independent of the data. For another
  character keep the dark belly circle (radius 270 px on the donut's center) and redraw the
  top of `Body`.
* `@marker` draws Marker Felt here; `--scale-x 70%` and `--scale-x 62%` narrow it into a
  tall hand-drawn face. With a real condensed hand face (Amatic SC Bold) drop `--scale-x`.

## 13. Variations and pairings

* **Another mascot:** any object with a round middle can hold the donut: a jar, a mug seen
  from above, a wheel, a globe, a pot. Keep it near-black or very dark so the ring glows,
  and give it a face only if the tone of the piece allows it.
* **Another hue family:** tomato (`#fbeee2` ground, `#3a1410` body, `#c8412b`, `#e9846b`,
  `#f5c9b6`), sea (`#eaf3f1`, `#0f2f33`, `#1f7a80`, `#62b5b0`, `#c5e3de`), or the "Forest
  report" palette in [../palettes.md](../palettes.md). Keep cream or a pale tint as ground.
* **Square (1080 x 1080):** title, mascot with the donut, and the three small donuts only;
  the list and the bottom row move to a second sheet.
* **Ranked reasons:** with more than five reasons, keep the mascot as a picture and replace
  the hero donut with sorted C-bars in the left column; or draw the top four and "Other".
* **Other small charts:** C-progress-ring for the three small donuts when each has one
  number that matters; C-icon-array in place of the bars for a ratio; C-stat-row in place
  of the two flags.
* **Wider bars:** when the bar module may take 35% W or more, use C-label-bars
  (`labelpos=inside` with the names in `labels=`, or `labelpos=above`) and drop the two text
  layers: the script then keeps names, bars, and values in step when the data changes.
* Relatives: i029 (a round plate as the pie's frame) and i011 (a giant eye whose iris is a
  pie) put the hero chart inside a picture the same way.

## 14. Pitfalls

* **Nine slices in one ring.** The original's hero donut has 9, four of them under 7%: the
  reader cannot rank them and three need leaders. Keep the five largest and fold the rest
  into "Other", or use sorted C-bars. Shares must add up to 100; say "rounded" in a note
  when the printed numbers add up to 99 or 101.
* **A donut for numbers that are not parts of one whole.** The original's third small donut
  prints one small rate per income band; they add up to far less than 100, yet the ring is
  full. Rates per group belong in C-bars or C-progress-bars, one bar per group.
* **Label bars that are sized to their labels.** The three bars must start at one edge and
  share one scale (`max=100`), even when a long label runs past a short bar. Never stretch
  a bar to fit its text.
* **A rate with no base.** "N% lower" needs "than whom" and a source in the same module or
  in a source line; so do the two flag values.
* **Poster-size small print.** The original's notes are 6 to 13 px at 1080 wide. Set
  nothing under 18 px (15 px for the source line), and cut copy instead: a 55-word
  paragraph becomes 20 words.
* **Rotated labels.** The original turns its two section labels 90 degrees. Readers tilt
  their heads; set them horizontal above their modules.
* **One hue, no contrast.** Neighboring slices need a clear step in lightness (pale next to
  dark), because there are no separators. Order the tones so that no two similar ones
  touch, or add `sep='#2e2d0e'`.
* **The mascot covers the data.** Keep at least 6% W of dark belly around the ring, keep the
  face above the donut, and let no arm or leader cross a slice label.
