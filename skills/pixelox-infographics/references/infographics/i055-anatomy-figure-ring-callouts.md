# i055: Anatomy figure with ring callouts and ribbon notes

## 1. Concept

A square explainer on a pale blue ground that maps four ideas onto a human body. A standing
figure in a dark suit fills the middle from top to bottom, drawn like an anatomy chart: the
brain shows through the head, the heart and lungs through the chest, bones through one arm,
muscle through one leg. A pale ring with a pictogram hangs on each of the four body parts by
a short connector, and each ring belongs to a text block: one in a tall pennant on the left,
three under ribbon headings on the right. The message is "a good practice has a head, a
heart, hands, and legs". It works because every abstract verb gets an address on the body,
the furniture is all one soft blue so the navy figure and its coral organs carry the
contrast, and coral returns only as a small bar beside every note, tying the text to the
body.

## 2. Data story

No numbers. An anatomy: 4 parts of one whole, each with a meaning, text only:

* 4 body parts (head, chest, hand, leg), each linked to 1 ring pictogram and 1 text block.
* 4 headings of 1 word (6 to 11 letters), caps.
* 8 notes, 2 per heading: the first says what the body part stands for (9 to 14 words on 2
  to 4 lines), the second gives one concrete action (18 to 28 words on 4 to 6 lines).
* 1 title of 6 to 7 words on 3 lines.
* 1 small mark or credit, bottom right.

Placeholder content in the recipe (invented, about running a volunteer team): head PLAN,
heart LISTEN, hands TEACH, legs DELIVER, with two notes each.

## 3. Information architecture

1. **Title**, top left: three slanted strips, read first because they are the only tilted
   thing on the page.
2. **The figure**, center: the map. Coral organs mark the four stops.
3. **Four rings**: one per stop, each on a connector that starts in a dot on the organ. The
   heart's ring goes left; the head's, the hand's, and the leg's rings go right.
4. **Left pennant**: the heart's text (the section the design treats as the most important:
   it gets the largest heading and its own panel).
5. **Right column**: three sections top to bottom in body order: head, hand, leg. Each is a
   ribbon heading and two notes with a coral bar.
6. **Mark**, bottom right.

The reader goes body part, ring, heading, notes; the rings are the hinges between picture
and text.

## 8. Charts and diagrams

No data chart. The diagram is an object with callouts:

* **Figure.** The recipe draws the body with **C-icon-array** used for a single unit:
  `scripts/waffle.js`, `shape=person value=1 total=1 cols=1`, box `32,8,36,86`, one color,
  `legend=none`. The pictogram is as wide as the box (389 px) and 2.15 times as tall: head
  center about 541, 210 (radius 79); torso x 384 to 696, y 314 to 648; legs x 452 to 532 and
  550 to 630, down to y 968. Organs are Lucide icons on top of it (section 9).
* **C-callout** x4: one `shape path` holds all four connectors (8 px, round caps), each
  from a dot (32 px `shape ellipse`) on the organ to the nearest point of its ring. The
  hand's connector is horizontal and crosses the torso; the others are short diagonals or
  near-horizontal lines. Leaders here are thick and pale, not hairlines: they are part of the
  ring.
* **C-icon-badge** x4, ring variant: `shape ellipse` with a lighter fill and a 16 to 18 px
  stroke; one or two icons inside. The second gear leaves its ring on the right, which
  keeps the ring from looking like a button.
* **C-ribbon-label** x3: strip 215 x 54 px, tails 48 px wide and 14 px lower with a 20 px
  notch, fold triangles under the strip's bottom corners. All six tails are one path, all
  six folds another.
* **Simplified:** the original figure is a detailed drawing (see section 9); the title
  ribbon has swallow tails and the three strips differ in length to fit their lines; the
  recipe uses plain strips with two fold triangles.

## 10. Effects and finish

None: flat color throughout, no shadows, no gradients, no outlines, no texture. Depth comes
from overlap only: rings over the figure, the heart's ring over the pennant's top edge, the
title strips over each other. The one transform is the title's rotation (`layer group`, then
`transform rotate --degrees=-13`).

## 11. Hierarchy and eye path

1. The figure: the darkest and largest shape, on the center axis.
2. The coral organs, then the pale rings beside them.
3. The slanted title, top left.
4. The pennant heading (50 px, white on blue), then the three ribbon headings (34 px).
5. The notes, each announced by its coral bar.

Headings are 2 to 3 times the notes (50 and 34 against 18). The notes are deliberately
quiet: the page works as a poster from the figure, rings, and five headings alone.

## 13. Variations and pairings

* **Another object:** any thing with nameable parts works as the figure: a bicycle, a house
  in cross-section, a tree (roots, trunk, crown), a product. For a rocket, bulb, or pencil
  use `scripts/object.js` and hang the rings on its band anchors.
* **Real anatomy or health:** keep the layout, replace the metaphor with facts (an organ,
  what it does, one number), and add C-source-line; then the positions must be anatomically
  right.
* **Symmetrical:** two pennants, left and right, two sections each, the rings alternating
  sides; drop the ribbon headings.
* **Portrait 4:5:** keep the figure and the rings in the top 70% and set the four sections
  as a 2 x 2 grid under them.
* **Dark:** ground `#16283a`, furniture `#27425c`, figure `#dbe7f0`, rings `#3f6f8f`, the
  same coral accents.
* Relatives: i038 and i020 (callouts on a rocket), i051 (a figure as the hub of six framed
  items). Pairs with C-icon-grid for a row of extra facts under the figure.

## 14. Pitfalls

* **A callout on the wrong part.** In an anatomy layout position is the data: a ring whose
  connector ends on the chest says "heart". Check every dot against the part its text
  names, in the same order top to bottom on both. For real anatomy, never move an organ to
  make room for a label. This is the data-integrity rule for every object-with-callouts
  design.
* **Pale type on the pennant.** The original sets `#ddf3ff` on `#9cc1dd` at about 14 px:
  nearly unreadable. Use white at 18 px or more on the mid blue, or darken the pennant to
  `#6f9cc4`.
* **Notes too small.** Tracked condensed caps look tidy at 14 px and cannot be read on a
  phone. Keep 18 px and cut words: 5 lines per note at most.
* **Crossing connectors.** Each ring goes to the side its text is on, and no two connectors
  cross. If two organs are close together, send one left and one right.
* **Too many stops.** Four to six callouts; with eight the rings cover the figure and the
  body stops reading as a body.
* **Rotating more than the title.** The tilt marks the title as a label stuck on the page;
  tilted notes or headings only look untidy. Keep the angle between 10 and 15 degrees.
