# i011: One-hue illustrated explainer around a giant eye

## 1. Concept

A tall illustrated argument in one hue: a white cloud carries the title, a giant gray-lidded
eye stares out of the upper half with a pie chart for an iris and the hero percentage in its
pupil, and five claims about one subject are set around it, each as a sentence that starts
with an ellipsis and ends in a number with its own small picture (a top hat, a stopwatch, a
row of figures, a small eye, a rising path). The message is "this thing works, and here are
five reasons with a number each". It works because the whole page is teal, white, and gray,
so the one aqua accent always marks the number or the key word, and because the eye, the
biggest shape, is itself the hero chart.

## 2. Data story

Six small facts, no long series:

* **1 hero share**, a large majority: a two-slice pie in the iris; the
  remainder is the lighter slice at the upper right, the number sits in the pupil.
* **1 share** (a second majority, a little smaller than the hero) printed on the hat, no
  chart.
* **1 multiplier** in the thousands ("N times faster"), a single bold number in a sentence.
* **1 ratio** of the form "N out of 5" as five figures: the counted ones are white, the rest
  aqua.
* **1 pair of shares compared in words** (a small minority against a large majority),
  beside a small eye whose iris is a second pie.
* **5 milestones without values** on a rising line: the heights are a picture of "growth",
  not data.

Placeholder data in the recipe (example data, invented, about paper notebooks): 65% still
remembered a week later (pie 65 to 35); 70% on the hat; "3x quicker"; 3 out of 5 people;
25% against 45% (small pie 45 to 55); five habits on the path.

## 3. Information architecture

1. **Title in the cloud**: a script lead-in of 3 words, then one compound word in tall
   condensed caps, its first half dark and its second half teal.
2. **Hero eye**, center: the pie iris, the number in the pupil, a three-line caption under
   the number.
3. **Claim 1**, left of the eye: lead-in caps, a phrase, a key word with a colon; a top hat
   with a number on it; a three-line caption.
4. **Claim 2**, right of the eye: the same three-part sentence, mirrored (right-aligned); two
   stopwatches; a four-line fact with one bold number.
5. **Claim 3**, lower left on the grid: sentence with two large display words and a ghost
   ampersand; a five-line note; a row of five figures; the ratio in words ("N out of 5")
   with a two-line caption.
6. **Claim 4**, lower right on a darker panel: sentence; a giant ghost "Aa"; a small eye; two
   short facts joined by "VS".
7. **Claim 5**, bottom: a two-line key word, then a thick white line that climbs from the
   lower left to the right edge through five ring nodes that grow in size, each with a
   short label.

Every claim reads "...verb phrase KEY WORD:" then its number, so the five blocks parse the
same way although no two look alike. The eye sends the reader left (hat), right (stopwatch),
then down both sides to the path.

## 8. Charts and diagrams

* **C-pie** (the iris): `scripts/donut.js` placed by center, `cx=50.6 cy=38.25 r=17.6`,
  `inner=0`, `values=65,35`, `start=90` (the large slice starts at 3 o'clock and runs
  clockwise, so the remainder ends at 3 o'clock in the upper right), `labelpos=none
  sep=none`. The pupil (a black disc of radius 9.1% W) covers the middle, so the pie reads
  as a thick ring; the share is printed in the pupil, the caption under it on the iris.
* **C-pie**, small (the second eye): `cx=73.06 cy=70.47 r=3.9`, `values=45,55`, aqua first.
* **C-icon-array**: `scripts/waffle.js`, `shape=person value=3 total=5`, white with
  `empty='#07d8b8'`, box `20,59.6,42,16.6`, `legend=none`; the ratio is set in type under it.
* **C-line** as a milestone path: `scripts/line.js`, `spark=1 markers=0 vlabels=none
  width=22`, white, box `-1.4,79.5,97,19.5` (the negative x puts the first point on the left
  edge), six values `0,25,49,49,100,100`: a start, two rises, a flat, a jump, a flat. The
  values only shape the climb. Ring nodes are `shape ellipse` layers with a white stroke
  centered on the logged anchors `p1.2` to `p1.6`; the grid zone and the right panel are
  `shape path` polygons whose lower edges run through the same anchors, so the dark ground
  shows under the line like the fill of an area chart.
* **C-stat-block** for the hat and stopwatch facts; **C-title-block** (two-part) in the cloud.
* Simplified: the script's figures are shorter than the original's long-legged ones and wear
  no glasses; node spacing is even (the original's is irregular).

## 9. Icons and illustration

* Top hat with rabbit ears (the picture for "turns it into a show"): built from `shape rect`
  (brim 190 x 18 px, band 133 x 37 px, buckle 22 x 37 px), a tapered `shape path` body, and
  one `shape path` with two tall ellipses for the ears.
* Stopwatches (the picture for speed): `timer`, 160 px, dark teal line with
  `--fill '#088d7c'`, 9 px line; a second `timer`, 110 px, gray with a pale gray fill. The
  original draws concentric teal rings on the big dial and a crown ring on top.
* Small eye (the picture for memory): rounded tile 180 x 150 px with radius 62, white ellipse
  154 x 100 px, pie, 34 px pupil. It repeats the hero in miniature.
* Pointer: a 5 px white line with round caps from the first figure toward the display word.
* Ghost glyphs: an ampersand and "Aa", one tone lighter or darker than their ground, used
  as pictures.
* Everything is flat fills in the page's own tones; no outlines.

## 10. Effects and finish

* Grid: `scripts/pattern.js kind=grid cell=60 weight=0.05`, placed over the grid zone and
  clipped to it with `layer clip`.
* Right panel shading: `fx overlay --kind gradient --stops '#064c4f@0,#066e67@0.55' --angle
  0` (dark at the left).
* No shadows, no textures, no outlines. Depth comes only from overlap: cloud over lids, lids
  over ground, path over everything.

## 11. Hierarchy and eye path

1. The title word in the white cloud (the tallest type, the highest contrast).
2. The eye and the aqua number in its black pupil.
3. Left to the hat number, right to the stopwatch's bold number.
4. Down the left side: display words, figures, the ratio in words.
5. Across to the right panel, then the white path from the lower left up to the big ring.

Title 165 px, hero number 96, hat and ratio numerals 60 to 68, display words 42, key words 26
to 28, facts 20.

## 13. Variations and pairings

* **Another hero object:** any round thing can hold the pie: a clock face, a plate, a lens,
  a porthole. Keep the number in a dark disc at its center.
* **Another hue:** build the same four steps from one color (navy `#0f2747`, `#1f4e8c`,
  `#2a6fb5`, accent `#7fd0ff`; or plum `#3a1840`, `#7a2f7f`, `#9c4aa0`, accent `#ffb3f0`).
  Keep white, gray, and black as they are.
* **Three claims:** drop claims 3 and 4 and bring the path up to 60% H for a 4:5 poster
  (1080 x 1350).
* **Real trend:** replace the picture path with a true C-area chart (a labeled axis, real
  values) when the last claim is about growth you can measure.

  C-timeline in place of the path.

## 14. Pitfalls

* **A picture path read as data.** The rising line has no axis and its heights were chosen
  for looks. Never print values on it; if growth is the claim, draw a real chart with a
  zero-based axis and a source.
* **The pie and its number disagree.** The remainder slice must be exactly 100 minus the
  number in the pupil: the script computes it from `values=`. Do not nudge the slice to make
  room for drawings.
* **More than two slices in the iris.** The pupil hides the center, so only the outer arc is
  read. Two slices work; with three or more use C-donut beside the eye.
* **"N out of 5" for a share that is not a multiple of 20%.** A figure row rounds hard (62%
  drawn as three figures of five). Use it only when the ratio is exact; otherwise print the
  percentage or use C-waffle.
* **A multiplier with no base.** "N times faster" needs "than what" in the same sentence and
  a source at the foot.
* **The accent everywhere.** If aqua also fills panels or the path, key words and numbers
  stop standing out. Keep it under 5% of the area.
* **Too many type families.** Four is the ceiling and each needs a single job; with fewer
  claims, drop the script and set the lead-in in the serif.
