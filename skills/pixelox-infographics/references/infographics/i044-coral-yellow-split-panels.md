# i044: Coral and yellow split panels with ring, people, and bulb

## 1. Concept

A landscape fact sheet made of only two flat colors that trade places: a coral column with a
yellow header on the left, and on the right a coral band over a yellow field. Each panel
holds one kind of content: three icon discs with text, a people icon array, one hero
percentage in a ring, two profile blurbs with round avatars, a light bulb filled to a level,
and a progress bar that leads into a closing sentence. The reader takes away "four numbers
and three themes about one subject, at a glance". It works because the two grounds do all
the framing (no boxes, no rules), every mark is the other panel's color or white, and each
number is drawn in a different small picture, so nothing has to be compared.

## 2. Data story

* **One hero percentage** (a clear majority), shown twice: inside a ring and as a big number
  beside it, with a bold line and a four-line paragraph. Six equal dots beside the number
  encode nothing.
* **Two groups counted in people**: 20 figures in rows of 7, 7, and 6; the first group dark,
  the second yellow (in the original the two groups are equal, and the second group uses a
  second pictogram); a two-key legend under it. Relationship: counted units, two parts of one whole.
* **One percentage as a bulb** filled from the bottom (most of the glass), the number
  printed in the empty top of the glass. No caption in the original.
* **One percentage as a two-tone bar** with the number at its right end. In the original the
  bar is filled clearly less far than the number beside it says: the picture and the number
  disagree (see Pitfalls).
* **Text**: a title of one caps word and one light word; a deck of about 20 words on 3 lines;
  three columns, each a lead of 5 or 6 words on 2 lines and a paragraph of 22 to 26 words
  on 11 short lines; two profile blurbs (a 3-word heading and about 20 words on 5 lines); a
  closing pair (6 words on 3 lines, 10 words on 4 lines). All of it is placeholder Latin in
  the original.

Placeholder data in the recipe (example data, invented: the public libraries of one town):

| Module | Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ring and hero number | 58% | residents who hold a library card |
| People icon array | 12 and 8 of 20 | adults and children among 20 visitors |
| Bulb | 70% | visitors who say they learned something new |
| Bar | 91% | loans returned on time |

## 3. Information architecture

1. **Header** (yellow, top left): title and deck. A zigzag edge tears it off from the column
   below.
2. **Three themes** (coral column): an icon disc, a two-line lead, and a long paragraph per
   column. Equal weight, read left to right.
3. **Who** (bottom of the column): the people icon array with its legend.
4. **Hero number** (coral band, top right): ring, big number, bold line, paragraph. The
   largest type on the page.
5. **Two profiles** (yellow field): a round avatar, a heading, a paragraph, twice, stacked.
6. **Bulb gauge**, right of the profiles: one level.
7. **Bar and closing**: a thin bar with its number, then a closing statement in two voices
   (a dark line on the left, bullets, a larger white line on the right).

The eye starts at the hero number (top right, biggest), drops to the title (top left, white
on yellow), then follows each panel downward. The color swap tells the reader where one
module ends: yellow things sit on coral, coral and dark things sit on yellow.

## 8. Charts and diagrams

* **C-progress-ring**: `scripts/donut.js`, `kind=ring value=58`, placed by center
  `cx=55.4 cy=15.6 r=8.2`, `inner=0.9` (a thin ring), `colors='#fcbc52'` (the value arc, from
  12 o'clock clockwise), `trackcolor='#ffffff'`, `round=0`, `center=' '` (no number from the
  script). A plain yellow `shape ellipse` of 216 px sits inside the ring and carries the
  number as `text add`. In the original the white remainder is drawn a little thicker than
  the yellow arc; the script draws both at one width.
* **C-icon-array**: `scripts/waffle.js`, `values=12,8 shape=person cols=7 gap=1.9`,
  `colors='#4b4548,#fcbc52'`, `labels='Adults,Children' legend=bottom`, `ink` and `muted`
  white, `fs=18`. `gap=1.9` spreads the figures to a pitch of about 87 px. 20 figures fill
  rows of 7, 7, and 6; the last row is left short, as in the original. The original gives
  the second group a second pictogram (a figure in a skirt); the script draws one figure in
  two colors, which is enough when the legend names them.
* **C-picture-gauge**: `scripts/object.js`, `kind=bulb value=70`, box `83.4,40.8,14.1,27.6`,
  `colors='#ffd966' empty='#e5e5e5' dark='#3f3f3f' shade=0`. The fill height is the value;
  the number goes in the empty part, 27 px above the logged `level` anchor. The original
  also draws a filament (two leads and a small coil in the darker yellow) and a four-stripe
  base; the script draws a plain glass and base.
* **C-progress-bars** with one bar: `scripts/bars.js`, `dir=h track=1 max=100 values=91`,
  box `45.4,77.4,39.9,3.8`, `gap=0.36` (a 30 px bar), `radius=0`, `colors='#f7706f'`,
  `trackcolor='#ffe599'`, `vlabels=0`, no `labels=`. The number is a separate `text add` at
  the track's right end. In the recipe the fill matches the number.
* **C-icon-badge** x3 (white line icons on yellow discs) and x2 (avatars on gray discs).
* **C-stat-block**: hero number, bold line, paragraph. **C-title-block** without rule.
* The six dots are decoration (plain `shape ellipse`). To make them count, draw ten with
  `scripts/waffle.js shape=circle value=5.8 total=10 cols=10` instead.

## 9. Icons and illustration

* Theme icons: white line icons, 112 px, 8 px line, centered on 186 px yellow discs:
  `clipboard`, `messages-square`, `settings`. The original shows the same three ideas (a
  clipboard, two speech bubbles, a gear) with rounded line ends.
* Avatars: the original has two flat illustrated busts in profile, facing right, cut off by
  the disc: rounded coral shoulders across the bottom third, a peach neck and face without
  features, bright yellow hair (short and curly on the first, long and straight on the
  second), no outlines. The recipe uses the `user` icon, 150 px, yellow 9 px line with a
  coral `--fill`, on the gray disc. For drawn avatars place `illustration.png` 200 px wide
  at each disc center (your image here) and clip it with `mask add --from-path` on a circle.
* Bulb: drawn by `scripts/object.js` (see section 8).
* Zigzag edge: one `shape path` with 16 teeth, 42 px wide and 20 px deep, written by hand.

## 10. Effects and finish

None: flat. No shadows, gradients, outlines, or textures; every edge is hard. The only
ornament is the zigzag edge under the header.

## 13. Variations and pairings

* **Portrait (1080 x 1500):** stack the panels: header and hero band on top (coral),
  the three icon columns (yellow), the people array (coral), profiles and bulb (yellow),
  bar and closing (coral). Keep the zigzag between the first two.

## 14. Pitfalls

* **A bar that disagrees with its number.** The original fills its bar several points short
  of the number printed beside it. Never draw the bar by hand: `values=` in
  `scripts/bars.js` sets the fill, and the text beside it must be the same number.
* **Numbers without a caption.** The original's bulb and bar carry a percentage and nothing
  else, so the reader cannot tell a share of what. Every gauge needs a noun: a caption
  under the bulb, a statement beside the bar. Add a source line at 98.5% H when the figures
  are real.
* **One value shown twice as if it were two.** The ring and the hero number repeat the same
  figure. That is fine as emphasis; two different numbers there would read as a
  contradiction.
* **Dots that look like a rating.** Six filled dots beside a percentage read as "6 of 6".
  Keep them clearly decorative (all one color, no empties) or make them count with
  `scripts/waffle.js shape=circle`.
* **Icon rows that do not say what one figure is.** 20 figures with a legend but no base
  ("20 visitors on a weekday") invite the reader to count and guess. Keep the legend with
  its counts, and keep the total at 20 or fewer.
* **Cream text on yellow.** The profile paragraphs in the original have a lightness
  difference of about 15 points from their ground and vanish on a phone. Use the dark slate
  `#474c51` there if the text matters, or set it at 24 px, semibold.
* **Eleven-line paragraphs in 185 px columns.** They are the weakest part of the original.
  Three or four lines per column and a larger people array read better.
