# i019: Pencil splitting into five labeled arrows

## 1. Concept

A square five-point graphic on pale gray. A fat yellow pencil leans up from the bottom left
corner; its barrel is made of five lengthwise strips in five ambers, and near the top the
strips peel apart and curl outward as five thick arrows, two to the left and three to the
right. One word runs along each arrow, and each arrow points at a small callout: a two-line
teal heading with a number 01 to 05, a few gray lines, and a hand-sketched pictogram. A thin
script word with a caps line and a five-color bar sits in the free corner at the bottom right.
The message is "one tool, five directions". It works because the split is literal: the reader
sees one object become five paths, follows each path by its own shade, and finds a word on the
way and a number at the end.

## 2. Data story

No numbers. Text only: five parallel items with no ranking and no sequence beyond the
numbering (01 at the top left, then down the page in a zig-zag).

* 5 arrow words, one each, 5 to 11 letters.
* 5 callouts: a heading on two lines (an ordinal word, then one generic word and a two-digit
  number) and a note (3 to 4 lines; the original's notes are placeholder text of about 12
  words; the recipe sets 8 to 10 words on 3 lines).
* 5 pictograms, one per callout.
* 1 title block: a script word of 4 or 5 letters, and a caps line of 2 or 3 words.

Placeholder content in the recipe (invented: five everyday uses of a pencil): arrow words
Thinking, Study, Sparks, Sharing, Numbers; headings FIRST / USE 01 to FIFTH / USE 05, each with
one sentence; title "notes" over "FIVE EVERYDAY USES".

## 3. Information architecture

1. **The pencil**, from the bottom left: the single source. Its tip touches the ground at
   23% W, 90% H; a soft shadow under the tip anchors it.
2. **Five arrows** peel off the barrel between 28% and 60% H. From the top: 01 up and to the
   left, 02 to the right, 03 to the left, 04 to the right, 05 to the right and lowest. The
   lowest arrow is the darkest (brown), the left ones the lightest and the darkest amber, so
   neighbors never share a shade.
3. **A word on each arrow**, set along its curve: the name of the item, read on the way out.
4. **Five callouts** at the arrow tips: on the left the pictogram is outside (toward the
   edge) and the text inside; on the right the text comes first and the pictogram is at the
   edge. So pictograms frame the page and text sits next to the arrowheads.
5. **Title block**, bottom right, quiet: it is a signature, not a headline. The five-color
   bar under it repeats the arrow shades as a key that this is one family.

There are no leaders: the arrows are the leaders. The numbers 01 to 05 give the order.

## 5. Layer stack

1. Ground: flat pale gray `#f2f2f2`.
2. Pencil tip: cream wood `#f1dec6` (a tan shade `#dbb373` on its left side in the original)
   and a dark graphite point `#3e3a34`.
3. Five strips, each 3.3% W wide with round ends. Where they meet the wood their round ends
   make a scalloped edge, like the paint line of a sharpened pencil. Along the barrel they lie
   side by side, left to right: yellow `#faca09` (03), orange-amber `#e39b01` (04), dark amber
   `#ca850d` (01), light amber `#f0a31c` (02), brown `#b46613` (05).
4. Each strip leaves the bundle in a smooth curve and ends in a triangular head of its own
   color. Strip 04 crosses over strips 01 and 02 on its way to the right, with a soft shadow
   under it.
5. Five words in dark shades of their strips (`#5e3a04` to `#7a5c00`), each following its
   arrow's curve, centered in the strip.
6. Floor shadow: black ellipse, blurred, 16% opacity.
7. Five pictograms: thin gray line drawings `#8f8f8f`.
8. Five headings in teal `#4b9ba6`; five notes in gray `#8a8a8a`.
9. Title block: script word `#9a9a9a`, caps line `#8d8d8d`, a bar of five segments in brown

## 8. Charts and diagrams

* **C-pencil**: `scripts/object.js`, `kind=pencil`, `bands=1`, `badges=none`, `rotate=23`,
  box `15.2,-11.07,57.02,104.7` (the box runs past the top edge on purpose: the pencil is
  1131 px long and only its lowest 320 px are shown). Scale 360 px per unit: barrel 180 px
  wide, wood cone 252 px long, tip at 247,976. The image is clipped to a ground-colored
  rectangle (`Window`, `190,660,360x322`) with `layer clip`, so the eraser end never shows:
  only the wood, the point, and a stub of barrel under the strips remain.
* **The strips and arrows** (not in the library; built by hand): five `shape path` strokes,
  36 px wide, `--cap round`. Each starts on the cone's base line (center 345,744; neighbors
  36 px apart along the direction 23 degrees below horizontal), runs straight up the barrel
  (direction 0.39,-0.92), and leaves it at its own height in one cubic curve that ends at the
  head's base: `M start L leave C c1 c2 base`. The head is a triangle 54 px long and 72 px
  wide. Leaving heights: 01 at y = 300, 02 at 320, 03 and 04 at 470, 05 at 640.
* **C-arc-label** for the five arrow words: `text add --align center`, then `text on-path`
  on a copy of the arrow's curve moved 8 px toward the lower side of the letters, so the word
  sits in the middle of the strip. For arrows that point left (01, 03) the curve is written
  from the head back to the barrel, so the word reads left to right.
* **C-icon-grid**, scattered: each callout is a pictogram, a heading, a note.
* **3D spec of the original pencil**: a six-sided pencil of five glossy lengthwise strips,
  seen from the side at eye level (elevation about 5 degrees), leaning 23 degrees, lit from
  the upper left. Each strip shows a light face toward the viewer (the hexes above) and a
  narrow darker face on its lower right, about 20% darker; the arrows carry a pale highlight
  along their upper edge; soft drop shadows fall where one arrow crosses another and under
  each head; the wood cone is cream `#f1dec6` with a tan shadow `#dbb373` on the left; the
  floor shadow lies to the right of the tip. Depth, thickness, and length encode nothing: the
  five strips are equal. The recipe is the flat equivalent with two drop shadows.

## 10. Effects and finish

* `fx drop-shadow` on the strip that crosses the others (04) and on the tallest one (01):
  offset 5,7, blur 14, color `#5a3a00`, opacity 30 to 35. This is what makes the strips look
  like separate ribbons.
* Round caps on the strips make the scalloped paint line on the wood.
* Floor shadow: `shape ellipse`, `filter gaussian --sigma 10`, `layer opacity` 16.
* The original adds a highlight gradient along every strip and a slightly lighter center to
  the ground; add `fx overlay --kind gradient` per strip (the color to a 15% lighter tint,
  `--angle 60`) if you want the gloss.

## 11. Hierarchy and eye path

1. The pencil and its fan of arrows: the only saturated mass, on the page's main diagonal.
2. The words on the arrows, read while following each curve outward.
3. The teal headings at the arrow tips, in number order from the top: left, right, left,
   right, right.
4. Pictograms at the edges; notes.
5. The script word, last.

Heading 32 and 24, arrow word 21, note 19: about 1.7 : 1.25 : 1.1 : 1.

Reuse:

* Layer order matters: `Window`, then `Pencil` clipped to it, then the strips with the
  crossing one (`Strand4`) last, then words, shadow, callouts.
* The geometry comes from three numbers: the tip (247,976), the lean (23 degrees), and the
  scale (360 px per unit, from the script's log). The cone's base center is 252 px up the axis
  from the tip (345,744); strip starts are that point moved by -72, -36, 0, 36, 72 px along
  (0.92, 0.39). Each strip then follows the axis direction (0.39, -0.92) until it leaves.
* To retarget an arrow, change only its last curve: the end point is the head's base (54 px
  behind the tip), the second control point lies 70 to 110 px behind the base on the arrow's
  final direction, the first control point 70 to 125 px past the leaving point on the axis.
  Then rebuild the head (tip, and two corners 36 px to each side of the base) and copy the
  curve into the word's `text on-path`, moved 8 px.
* Change first: the five arrow words, the five headings and notes, the five icon names, the
  script word and the caps line. With 3 or 4 items drop strips from the outside in and keep
  the barrel 36 px per strip.

## 13. Variations and pairings

* **Relatives:** i036 (a light bulb whose rim opens into a fan of five colored panels) and
  i026 (a bulb as the hub of fifteen icon nodes) carry the same "one idea, many branches"
  story with a bulb; this one is the only design in the set where the object itself turns
  into the connectors. The plain alternative is C-pencil lying down with five numbered bands
  (a sequence, where this is a set of directions).
* **A sequence instead of a set:** keep the pencil and send all five arrows to the right,
  one above the other, tips on one vertical line at 68% W; the callouts become a numbered
  list and the piece reads top to bottom.
* **Other objects that can split:** a paint brush, a cable, a road, a tree trunk. The recipe
  stays the same: a clipped object for the base, strokes for the branches.
* **Palette swaps:** five greens from `#b7e07a` to `#2f6b3a` with a coral heading color for
  growth topics; five blues from `#9fd3f5` to `#1f4e8c` with an amber heading color for
  technology. Keep one hue in five lightness steps and one opposite accent for headings.
* **With numbers:** set a figure as heading line 2 ("42%") and the label as line 1; the arrow
  word names the category. Pairs with C-source-line across the bottom when figures appear,
  and with C-title-block on top in a 4:5 canvas when the piece needs a real headline.

## 14. Pitfalls

* **Arrow size read as amount.** A longer, thicker, or higher arrow looks like "more". All
  five strips have one width and the heads one size; lengths differ only because of the
  layout. If the items have values, do not scale the arrows: print the value in the heading
  and add a C-bars chart for the comparison. This is the data-integrity rule for this piece.
* **Upside-down words.** `text on-path` sets letters to the left of the path's direction. On
  an arrow that points left, write the word's path from the head toward the barrel, or the
  word hangs under the strip and reads backward.
* **Long words on tight curves.** Letters fan apart on a bend. One word of up to 9 letters,
  21 px, placed on the flattest part of the curve (move it with `--offset`).
* **The eraser showing.** The script draws a whole pencil. Without the clipped `Window` the
  ferrule and eraser stick out above the arrows; without the negative box top the pencil
  shrinks to fit the canvas and the strips no longer cover the barrel.
* **Shades too close.** Five ambers work only if neighbors differ: order the strips so light
  and dark alternate along the barrel, and keep at least 8 points of lightness between any
  two that touch. For readers who cannot rely on shade, the word on each arrow is the
  label: never drop it.
* **Notes that touch the arrowheads.** Text blocks here are only 12% W wide. Keep notes to 18
  characters a line and 20 px clear of every head, or move the callout outward rather than
  shrinking the type under 18 px.
