# i046: Dark space rocket over four folded paper columns

## 1. Concept

A square four-part graphic on a dark gray-green sky. A silver rocket with a red nose stands on
the center axis; four paper ribbons (yellow, orange, teal, blue) leave its nozzle, fan out down
a slope, fold over an edge, and hang as four upright columns numbered 01 to 04 that run off
the bottom of the page. Beside the rocket sit four callouts, two on each side: a round dark
badge with an icon, a one-word title in one of the four colors, a short note, and a thin
leader that drops toward the fan. A blue globe is cut by the top right corner and a pink
planet by the left edge. The message is "four parts carry this launch". It works because each
color is one unbroken strip from the rocket to its number, and the same color names its
callout, so the reader matches note to column without a legend; the dark ground makes the
four paper colors the only bright things on the page.

## 2. Data story

No numbers. Text only, an ordered set of four:

* 4 parts, each with a column (a number 01 to 04, a small one-word tag, a bold one-word
  label) and a callout (a one-word title, a note of 15 to 20 words on 4 lines in the
  original; the recipe sets 8 to 10 words on 3 lines at twice the size).
* 4 pictograms, one per callout.
* 1 two-part title on one line: 2 words in pale blue, then 1 to 3 words in white.

Placeholder content in the recipe (invented, about preparing a product launch): PROOF, TEAM,
PLAN, BUILD, each with one sentence; title "LAUNCH PLAN" and "IN FOUR PARTS".

## 3. Information architecture

1. **Title**, top center-right, small and light: it names the set and stays out of the way.
2. **The rocket**, center: the goal. Nothing is written on it.
3. **Four ribbons and columns**: the list itself. Order runs left to right: 01 yellow, 02
   orange, 03 teal, 04 blue. The white numbers are the largest type on the page.
4. **Four callouts** beside the rocket, matched to the columns by color: top left yellow
   (01), bottom left orange (02), top right teal (03), bottom right blue (04). So the reading
   order is down the left side, then down the right side. Each callout's leader leaves a dot
   beside its badge, runs toward the rocket, turns down along the rocket's side, and ends in
   a dot just above the fan.
5. **Globe, planet, stars**: scene setting. They fill the two corners the callouts leave empty
   (top right, lower left).

## 4. Format and grid

* Square. Recipe canvas 1080 x 1080. One vertical axis at 50% W; left and right mirror each
  other.
* Title: one line from 42% to 76% W, baseline at 6.1% H.
* Rocket: nose tip at 25.9% H, red collar at 51 to 55% H. Body 14% W wide (12.3% W from the
  script with `aspect=1.5`); fins reach 41.2% and 58.8% W (40% and 60% W in the recipe).
* Ribbon stem under the collar: 6.7% W wide (the original: 8.4% W), four stripes, from 53.7%
  to 58% H.
* Fan: from the stem's foot (58% H) to the fold (73.8% H); at the fold it is 50.4% W wide
  (24.8% to 75.2% W).
* Columns: four of 12.6% W each, `24.8%,73.8%,50.4% x 26.2%`, running off the bottom edge.
* Callout badges: discs of radius 6% W, centers at 26.5% and 73.5% W, 32.6% and 54.6% H.
  Text blocks: left blocks flush right on 18.7% W, right blocks flush left on 81.3% W; outer
  margins 2% W. Titles start 3.7% H above the badge center.
* Leaders: start dots at 34% and 66% W on the badge centers' heights; the upper pair turns
  down at 38.1% and 61.9% W and ends at 59% H; the lower pair turns at 36.5% and 63.5% W and
  ends at 61% H.
* Globe: center 91% W, 9% H, radius 13.3% W (three quarters visible). Planet: center 6.7% W,
  76.5% H, radius 8% W (a sliver cut by the left edge).

## 8. Charts and diagrams

* **C-rocket**: `scripts/object.js`, `kind=rocket`, `aspect=1.5` (the short, fat rocket: one
  and a half times the script's normal width), `bands=2`, `badges=none`, `flame=0`, box
  `36,25.9,28,35.8`, `colors='#df5352,#d9dfe0'` (nose, body), `fincolor` and `dark` both
  `#bc3835` so fins and nozzle read as one red collar. The image is placed twice: once under
  the fan as the caster of the long shadow, once on top. The porthole is two native discs
  high on the body: the script's own (`window=1`) sits lower, in the middle of the body
  band, and `aspect=` widens it into an oval. Simplified: the original rocket is egg-shaped
  (body 14% W by 24% H) with a rounded metallic gradient, a row of rivets under the
  porthole, and two small rounded fins; the script draws a flat straight-sided rocket with
  one light strip and larger swept fins.
* **The ribbons (3D spec)**: not a chart; four `shape path` trapezoids and eight rectangles.
  Object: four paper strips side by side. Each leaves the nozzle as a narrow ribbon, widens
  down a ramp, bends over a horizontal fold at 73.8% H, and hangs straight down. View: from
  the front (rotation 0 degrees), camera raised about 30 to 35 degrees, so the ramp is seen
  from above and the hanging part face on. Light: from above and slightly from the left. Face
  colors per strip: ramp (top face, lit) `#f9eb6d`, `#f0a050`, `#41cbcc`, `#4894c7`; hanging
  face (front) `#fbd362`, `#e29149`, `#3ebbc0`, `#3a81b1`; a soft shadow under the fold, 5%
  H deep, `#eaa847`, `#c96f38`, `#229a9c`, `#27668a`; the stem beside the nozzle in shade
  `#c6a14a`, `#be7b3f`, `#2f9fa4`, `#31739f`. No side faces show. Depth and width encode
  nothing: the four columns are equal. Flat build: trapezoid N joins stripe N to column N;
  `fx overlay` with a three-stop gradient (`--angle 90`) darkens the top 22% of each column.
* **C-globe**: `scripts/geo.js`, run with `render d3`, `projection=globe`, `rotate=-95,35`,
  box `77.7,-4.35,26.67,26.67` (the square around a sphere of radius 13.3% W with its center
  at 91% W, 9% H; the box reaches past the top and right edges, so the corner cuts the
  sphere), `ocean='#164d73'`, `land='#dcf1f6'`, `stroke=none`, `graticule=0`, shading on.
  **Map spec**: region set: all land as one silhouette, no borders; projection: an
  orthographic globe centered near 35 degrees north, 95 degrees west (one continent and the
  ocean east of it face the viewer); encoding: none, it is scenery; labels, legend, insets:
  none. Light: the original has a pale glossy highlight from the upper right and a thin
  lighter rim `#38677d` on the lower left; the script lights the sphere from the upper left.
  Fallback for builds before 0.6.0: a `shape ellipse` disc in `#164d73` with a diagonal
  `fx overlay` gradient and the `earth` line icon (346 px, `#a9cfe0`, 8 px line) on the same
  center, sized so the icon's ring is the disc's rim.
* **Planet (3D spec)**: a sphere of radius 8% W, pink, lit from the top left, glossy; flat
  build: a disc with a three-stop diagonal gradient.
* **C-icon-badge** x4 (filled disc variant, translucent black) and **C-callout** x4 with a
  rounded elbow: `M x0 y0 L x1 y0 Q xc y0 xc y0+12 L xc y1`, corner radius 12 px.

## 10. Effects and finish

* **Long shadows** (the mark of this style): `fx long-shadow --angle 315` on the rocket
  (length 30% of the diagonal, opacity 30, fade 100), the planet (22%), and each badge (9%,
  opacity 40). All fall down to the right and fade to nothing. Keep one angle for all.
* **Fold shadow** on the columns: `fx overlay --kind gradient --angle 90` with the column
  color up to 78% of the height and the darker tone at the top.
* **Sphere shading**: the globe script's own highlight and shadow (`shade=1`, the default);
  an `fx overlay` gradient on the planet (`--angle 135`).
* **Ground**: a linear `fill gradient` from the top left to the bottom right corner.
* No outlines. The original adds a faint paper grain and a soft drop shadow under each
  ribbon edge; leave them out, or add `filter grain` to a stamped copy at low strength.

2. Up the ribbons to the rocket.
3. Out to the four colored titles, left pair, then right pair, pulled by the leaders.
4. Notes, then the title line at the top (small and thin on purpose).
5. Globe and planet frame the corners.

## 13. Variations and pairings

* **Closest relative: i040.** The same skeleton (rocket on the axis, four colored stripes
  that fan out, numbers 01 to 04) on a white ground with a pale world map: there the fan
  lands on a low row of icon cells and the four callouts sit beside the fan with numbers and
  diagonal leaders. This one is the dark, three-dimensional version: the fan folds into tall
  columns that carry the numbers, and the callouts move up beside the rocket with icon
  badges. Take i040 for a report page, this one for a slide or a cover.
* **Other rockets:** i038 cuts the rocket itself into four numbered bands with callouts on

## 14. Pitfalls

* **Columns that look like a bar chart.** Four colored columns side by side invite a
  comparison of heights. Keep them equal and let them run off the bottom edge so no top or
  bottom can be compared; if the parts have amounts, print the figures in the columns and
  say the unit, or use C-columns on a real zero-based axis. This is the data-integrity rule
  for this piece.
* **Callout colors that do not match the columns.** The color is the only link between a
  note and its column. Yellow top left must be column 01, and so on; never recolor a title
  for contrast without recoloring its column, leader, and dots.
* **Dark blue type on the dark ground.** The fourth color `#3a81b1` is fine as a column but
  too dark for a title on `#2a3131`: the recipe lifts the title and the leader to `#5aa6de`.
  Check every colored title for a lightness gap of 50 points to the ground behind it; the
  ground is much lighter at the top left than at the bottom right.
* **Turned text.** The original turns the column text a quarter turn; a label longer than
  one word then takes a tilted head to read. Set it upright as the recipe does, or keep a
  turned label to one short word and never turn the notes.
* **Shadows in several directions.** Long shadows work only when all fall at one angle
  (315 degrees here) and fade out. A drop shadow straight down on one element next to them
  breaks the light.
* **The rocket's shadow over the ribbons.** If the shadow lies on top of the fan it dirties
  the yellow and the orange. Put the shadow-casting copy of the rocket under the fan.
