# i051: Figure hub with six bracketed icon modules

## 1. Concept

A square icon sheet on white in two colors, orange and dark gray. A one-line title in thin
gray capitals runs edge to edge over an orange rule. In the middle stands a faceless player,
head and shoulders, in a yellow shirt: the only multi-colored thing on the page. Six modules
surround him, two above, one on each side, two below; each is a two-tone pictogram, an orange
one-word title, and a short centered note, held by a thin orange bracket whose open side
faces away from the figure. A dashed orange line with a dot at its end ties every bracket to
the figure, and a strip of sixteen small pictograms closes the page. The message is "one
subject, six things that belong to it". It works because the brackets do the grouping that
boxes would do with far more ink, and because two colors plus one yellow figure leave no doubt
where the center is.

## 2. Data story

No numbers. One subject with six facets of equal weight and no order (relationship: hub and
members):

* 1 title of 3 words and an ampersand, about 29 characters, one line.
* 1 hub: a figure with no label.
* 6 modules. Each: a pictogram, a title of 1 word (4 to 7 letters), and a note of about 19
  words on 7 centered lines of 17 to 22 characters.
* 1 strip of 16 unlabeled pictograms from the same family; 6 of them repeat the modules'
  pictograms at a small size.

The original's notes are filler text; only the pattern matters.

Placeholder content in the recipe (invented, about a children's match day): WARM-UP,
SIGNALS, COACH, KIT, PRIZES, CROWD, each with a note of 15 to 18 words on 6 lines; title "SIX
THINGS A MATCH DAY NEEDS".

## 3. Information architecture

1. **Title bar**: the subject, full width, with a rule under it.
2. **Hub**: the figure, dead center. It answers "who is this about".
3. **Six modules** around it. Top pair and bottom pair: pictogram on the left, title and note
   on the right. Side pair: pictogram on top, then title, then note, all centered. Each
   module answers "what is this thing and why does it matter".
4. **Connectors**: dashed lines from the middle of each bracket to a dot beside the figure:
   diagonal for the four corner modules, horizontal for the two side modules. They say "this
   belongs to him".
5. **Icon strip**, under a second rule: the whole pictogram family in one row. It works as a
   footer or an index; on a set preview it is the inventory.

There is no reading order among the modules. Western readers start top left and go round
clockwise; the symmetric layout makes any order work.

## 4. Format and grid

* Square. Recipe canvas 1080 x 1080. The title and the strip run to within 2% W of the edges;
  the modules keep 2% W on the sides.
* Vertical zones in %H: title 2.8 to 7.5; rule at 9.8; modules 13 to 89; rule at 90.9; strip
  92 to 98.
* A 3 x 3 arrangement with the figure in the middle cell and the four corner cells merged
  into the top and bottom pairs:
  * top pair: `19.8%,13%,28.5% x 20%` and `51.7%,13%,28.5% x 20%`;
  * side pair: `2%,34%,22% x 32%` and `76%,34%,22% x 32%`;
  * bottom pair: `19.8%,69.5%,28.5% x 20%` and `51.7%,69.5%,28.5% x 20%`;
  * figure: `37.5%,37.8%,25% x 26.5%`.
* Brackets: the top pair's run along the modules' bottom edge (line at 32.7% H, both ends
  curling up 4.4% H with a corner radius of 4% W); the bottom pair's along their top edge
  (69.5% H, ends curling down); the side pair's are vertical lines at 23.8% and 76.2% W from
  36.9% to 66% H, ends curling outward 4.6% W.
* Connector dots (radius 0.85% W) at 42.8%,41.8% and 57.2%,41.8% (top), 43.3%,51.2% and
  56.6%,51.2% (sides), 37.5%,65% and 62.4%,65% (bottom).
* Strip: 16 pictograms, each 5.5 to 6% W across, on a pitch of 6.1% W from 3.5% to 96.5% W,
  centered at 95% H.
* The whole page is mirror-symmetric about 50% W.

## 5. Layer stack

1. Ground: white, flat.
2. Title: gray `#5e5f61` capitals, one line.
3. Two full-width orange `#f3841c` rules, 3 px, at 9.8% and 90.9% H.
4. Six brackets: orange lines 4 px thick, no fill, round corners.
5. Six connectors: orange dashed lines 4 px thick (dash 12, gap 9), each ending in an orange
   dot of radius 9 px beside the figure.
6. Figure: a head-and-shoulders bust drawn in flat shapes, split down the middle into a
   light and a shaded half: skin `#fbbc87` and `#e3a877`, hair `#b5722f`, shirt yellow
   `#f4eb3b` and `#d6d030`, a green `#188446` V collar and sleeve cuffs, a small round emblem
   on the chest. No eyes, no mouth, no number, no name.
7. Six module pictograms: solid two-tone drawings in gray `#4d4f4e` and orange, 10 to 14% W
   across.
8. Six module titles in orange and six notes in dark gray `#4d4d4d`.
9. Strip: 16 small two-tone pictograms.

original's, and notes are 17 on 6 lines so they stay readable.

## 8. Charts and diagrams

* No data chart. The diagram is a hub with six members, the C-hub-spokes idea, but the
  generator (`scripts/cycle.js`, `style=spokes`) puts round nodes on a ring with a title and
  note outside each; it cannot draw bracketed modules with a pictogram beside a text block,
  or two modules side by side above and below the hub. So the recipe builds the modules from
  furniture:
  * **C-icon-grid** cells: pictogram, title, note (six times, in two layouts);
  * **C-callout** in its dashed form for the connectors: `shape line --dash 12,9`, a dot at
    the hub end (one `shape path` holds all six dots);
  * brackets: `shape path` with two quadratic corners, for example
    `M 214 306 Q 214 353 262 353 L 474 353 Q 522 353 522 306` for the top left one;
  * **C-title-block** in its centered form with a full-width rule.
* Geometry is mirrored, not computed: every right-hand x is 1080 minus the left-hand x.
* No axis, no legend, no values. Module size and position encode nothing.
* Simplified: the original's pictograms are solid drawings (see section 9), the recipe's are
  line icons with an orange fill; the figure is rebuilt from ten flat shapes.

## 10. Effects and finish

None: flat color, no shadows, no gradients, no texture. The only modelling is the figure's
vertical split into a light and a shaded half, done with a second shape over the right half.
Dashes on the connectors (`--dash 12,9`) and round bracket corners (48 px) are the only
ornaments.

## 11. Hierarchy and eye path

1. The figure: the only yellow and green, in the middle, with six lines pointing at it.
2. The title: the largest type, but thin and gray, so it frames rather than shouts.
3. The orange module titles, clockwise from the top left, each with its pictogram.
4. The notes.
5. The strip, as a footer.

Title 70 px, module titles 37, notes 15. Pictograms are 2 to 2.5 times the height of a module
title: picture first, name second, note third in every module.

Notes for reuse:

* **Change first:** the title (`--fit-width 1040` sizes any one-line title of 22 to 32
  characters; shorter titles get too tall, so set `--size 62` then), the six module titles
  and notes, the six pictogram names, and the strip's sixteen names.
* **Module anatomy.** Top and bottom pairs: pictogram centered 151 px left of the note's
  axis (275 and 426; 627 and 779, and the same on the bottom row with 291 and 434; 635 and
  781); title cap top 53 px above the note. Side pair: pictogram, title, and note share one
  axis (139 on the left, 939 on the right). Notes are 6 lines of at most 21 characters at 17
  px; a longer note runs into its bracket.
* **Brackets and connectors.** Each bracket is 308 px long (the side ones 314 px tall) with
  48 px corners. A connector starts at the bracket's middle and stops at a dot 20 to 30 px
  from the figure. Mirror every x about 540 for the right-hand side.
* **Your own figure:** see section 9. Keep its box (270 x 286 px, centered on 540,552) so the
  six dots stay just outside it.
* **Four modules:** drop the side pair and move the dots of the corner pairs to the figure's
  shoulders. **Eight modules** do not fit this layout: use C-icon-grid in two rows of four.
* **Strip:** 16 icons on a pitch of 65.6 px from x = 48; for 12 icons use a pitch of 89 px.
  Leave the strip out when it would only repeat the modules.

## 13. Variations and pairings

* **Product at the center:** `product.png` in the figure's box, six bracketed features
  around it, no strip. The brackets then act as callouts on a product shot.
* **One accent swap:** keep the gray, change orange to the subject's color (`#0d9488` for
  health, `#2563eb` for technology); change the figure's shirt so it is still the only third
  color.
* **Portrait 4:5 (1080 x 1350):** add 135 px between the top pair and the figure and between
  the figure and the bottom pair, lengthen the side brackets, and give each note a seventh
  line.
* **With numbers:** put one figure per module in place of the title ("11", "90 min") at 34
  px orange and move the name into the note's first line; add a source line above the strip.
* **Relatives:** i026 and i036 are hubs around a light bulb (many small nodes on thin lines;
  five solid panels). Pairs with C-stat-row in place of the strip and with C-process-row on a
  second page when the six things happen in an order.

## 14. Pitfalls

* **Boxes in place of brackets.** Closing each module into a full frame doubles the ink and
  makes six cards fight the figure. One bracket per module, on the side that faces the hub.
* **Connectors that cross a module or the figure.** Each line leaves the middle of its
  bracket and stops at a dot short of the figure; diagonals at about 45 degrees for the
  corner pairs, horizontals for the sides. Never route a line through a note.
* **A third color in the modules.** Yellow, green, and skin tone belong to the hub. One
  yellow pictogram and the figure stops being the center.
* **Mixed pictogram styles.** Solid two-tone drawings beside thin line icons look like two
  sources. One treatment for all 22 pictograms (here: gray line, orange fill).
* **The layout claims equality (data integrity).** Six modules of the same size around one
  hub say "six equal parts of this subject". Do not use the layout for items that are ranked
  or that differ in size, and do not add numbers to some modules and not to others. If a
  module carries a figure, every module needs one, with its unit, and the sheet needs a
  source line above the strip.
* **A recognizable person.** The figure is generic on purpose: no face, no number, no real
  crest or kit. A real athlete needs their own photo and permission, and a different design
  (a stat poster).
* **Notes at the original's size.** 15 px is the floor at 1080 wide; below that the notes
  turn into gray texture. Cut words and keep 17 px.
